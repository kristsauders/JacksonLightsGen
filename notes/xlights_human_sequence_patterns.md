Measured empirically (2026-08-09) from four finished, human-made sequences in the 2026 show folder: `Biometrix - Silent Night` (bass music — closest stylistic match to CloZee/LSDREAM material), `01 - Cold Heart (PNAU Remix) 2025 40fps`, `Blinding Lights 2025 40fps`, `Chappell Roan - Pink Pony Club 2025 40fps`. **These numbers are the target spec — compare any generated sequence against them before shipping it.** Analysis scripts live in `xlights-mcp-server/custom_tools/` (`study_sequences.py`-style ad-hoc scripts were used; re-derive if needed).

## The single biggest structural insight: target GROUPS, not individual models

Human sequences place effects on **45–57 total elements**, of which roughly half to three-quarters are **model groups** (`Arches`, `Orbs`, `Curtains All`, `Icicles`, `Tunnel`, `C9s`, `Candy Canes`, `Pixel Tree Forest`, ...), not on individual models. A group appears in the `.xsq` as an ordinary `<Element type="model" name="<group name>">` — same form as a model element; xLights resolves it as a group because that name exists in `<modelGroups>` in `xlights_rgbeffects.xml`. Generating per-model effects across all 235 models is fundamentally the wrong shape and is what makes output feel like isolated props rather than a designed show.

**You cannot invent a group in the `.xsq` alone** — a name that isn't a real model or group renders as an orphan/stale element (real examples of stale ones exist in `Blinding Lights`: `12in Star 1`, `36EFLake`). To target a new combination, the group must be added to `xlights_rgbeffects.xml` — that's the user's layout file, so ask before modifying it.

## Buffer style decides "spanning" vs "duplicated" — this was the curtain bug

`B_CHOICE_BufferStyle` on an effect controls how a group's members map into the render buffer:
- **`Per Preview`** — the whole group maps to its real 2D layout as ONE canvas, so an effect sweeps continuously across all members. This is what makes a chase span the garage curtain instead of repeating on each panel. Used 746× for `Curtain`, and on most `SingleStrand` chases in Silent Night.
- **`As Pixel`** — each member model becomes a single pixel in the buffer. **This is the clean, native way to do a cross-model chase** across scattered props (floods, orbs, mini trees): put `Bars` or a chase on the group with `As Pixel` and it steps prop-to-prop. Far better than my hand-rolled per-model staggered-burst hack.
- **`Per Model *`** (`Per Model Default`, `Per Model Single Line`, `Per Model Per Preview`) — repeats the effect independently on every member. **This is what causes "duplicated instead of spanning."** `engine.py`'s stock Chase presets hardcode `Per Model Single Line`, which is why grouped curtains still looked duplicated.
- `Default` / `Single Line` — group treated as one long strand.

## Whole-area container models vs per-controller panels

This layout deliberately contains **overlapping** models at two levels of granularity (this is what Krists means about model list order and "most specific on top"):
- Container/whole-area models have **no `Controller` attribute and `NumStrings` > 1**: `Curtain Front` (3 strings), `Curtain Garage` (3), `Curtain North` (2), `Curtain South`.
- Their panels have `NumStrings=1` and their own controller: `Curtain Front Center/Left/Right`, `Curtain Garage 1`, `Curtain Garage-2..5`, `Curtain North Left/Right`, etc.

Human sequences target the **container** models for spanning effects. Detection heuristic that works here: a model with empty `Controller` and `NumStrings > 1` is a container; any model whose name starts with the container's name followed by a space or hyphen is one of its panels — exclude those panels from the cast and use the container. Never light both levels at once, they physically overlap.

## Density / staging — generated output was 5–17× too dense

| metric | human sequences | my v5 generated |
|---|---|---|
| total effect placements | 2,438 – 8,487 | 42,934 |
| elements carrying effects | 45 – 57 | 235 |
| mean simultaneous lit elements | 40 – 112 | ~all |
| **mean lit fraction of display** | **11 – 26 %** | ~100 % |
| layers per element | mean 2.1–3.9, max 6–12 | 3–4 |

Occupancy swings enormously over a song — Silent Night goes 7 → 37 → 98 → 11 → 115 → 8 elements across its runtime. **That dynamic range IS the drama.** A wall-to-wall ambient bed on every model in every section (what the old generator did "so nothing is ever dark") is precisely the wrong instinct and is the root of "too dense / everything on all the time / not enough drama."

## Effect durations are SHORT — this drives the rhythmic feel

Duration distribution (Silent Night, n=3673): **≤150ms: 12%, 150–400ms: 30%, 400ms–1s: 28%, 1–3s: 26%, 3–10s: 4%, >10s: 0.2%.** Median across sequences is 250–825ms. Roughly 70% of all effects are under one second.

Crucially, chases use **`E_TEXTCTRL_Chase_Rotations=1.0`** almost universally — the rhythm comes from placing many *short* one-rotation effects on beats, **not** from long effects with a high internal rotation count. This corrects an earlier wrong conclusion of mine (I'd measured `Rotations=8` on one Cold Heart layer and built a `scale_rotations()` helper that scaled rotations up with block duration — that produced the "arches move way too fast / blurry" complaint). **Prefer short effects with rotations=1 over long effects with many rotations.**

## Validated effect settings (copy verbatim — never guess `E_`/`B_` key names)

A wrong parameter key does not error, it silently no-ops. Extract real settings strings from these sequences' `EffectDB` instead of inventing them.

- **Curtain** (dramatic open/close, 746 uses): `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=Per Preview,E_CHECKBOX_Curtain_Repeat=0,E_CHOICE_Curtain_Edge=middle,E_CHOICE_Curtain_Effect=close,E_SLIDER_Curtain_Swag=0,E_TEXTCTRL_Curtain_Speed=1.0` (also `Curtain_Effect=open`)
- **Wave** (great for icicles/linear props, 1265 uses): `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=Default,B_CHOICE_BufferTransform=None,E_CHECKBOX_Mirror_Wave=0,E_CHOICE_Fill_Colors=Palette,E_CHOICE_Wave_Direction=Left to Right,E_CHOICE_Wave_Type=Triangle,E_SLIDER_Number_Waves=1202,E_SLIDER_Thickness_Percentage=40,E_SLIDER_Wave_Height=50,E_SLIDER_Wave_YOffset=0,E_TEXTCTRL_Wave_Speed=50.00,T_CHOICE_LayerMethod=1 reveals 2`
- **Bars cross-prop sweep**: `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=As Pixel,E_CHECKBOX_Bars_3D=0,E_CHECKBOX_Bars_Gradient=0,E_CHECKBOX_Bars_Highlight=0,E_CHOICE_Bars_Direction=Right,E_SLIDER_Bars_BarCount=1,E_TEXTCTRL_Bars_Cycles=16.0`
- **SingleStrand chase** (dominant effect overall): `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=Per Preview,E_CHECKBOX_Chase_Group_All=0,E_CHOICE_Chase_Type1=<From Middle|To Middle|Left-Right|Right-Left>,E_CHOICE_Fade_Type=<Head and Tail|None>,E_NOTEBOOK_SSEFFECT_TYPE=Chase,E_SLIDER_Number_Chases=1,E_TEXTCTRL_Chase_Rotations=1.0`
- **Shockwave** (1220 uses): `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=Default,E_CHECKBOX_Shockwave_Blend_Edges=1,E_CHECKBOX_Shockwave_Scale=0,E_NOTEBOOK_Shockwave=Position,E_SLIDER_Shockwave_Accel=0,E_SLIDER_Shockwave_CenterX=50,E_SLIDER_Shockwave_CenterY=50,E_SLIDER_Shockwave_Cycles=1,...`
- **Ripple** (implode/explode): `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=Per Preview,E_CHOICE_Ripple_Movement=<Explode|Implode>,E_CHOICE_Ripple_Object_To_Draw=Circle,E_SLIDER_Ripple_Thickness=3,E_TEXTCTRL_Ripple_Cycles=1.0`
- **Off** is used explicitly as an effect (`X_Effect_Locked=True`) to force darkness — a real tool for drama, not just absence of effects.
- **Warp** requires `T_CHECKBOX_Canvas=1` to affect layers below it.
- **Meteors** icicle-drip: `B_CHOICE_BufferStyle=Vertical Stack,E_CHOICE_Meteors_Effect=Icicles,...`

Note also `Shader` (GLSL) is used heavily in Silent Night (790 uses) — needs external shader files, skipped for now but explains some of that sequence's look.

## Submodels are a major addressable layer, and the group parser was silently dropping them

Discovered 2026-08-09 (v7 round) responding to "use the window outlines / snowflake arms and petals." xLights addresses a model's sub-region as `"ParentModel/SubmodelName"` (e.g. `Front Center Window Matrix/Verticals`, `Snowflake 1/Outline`, `GE 7.5 Flake Lotto/Spinner 1`), and real sequences use this **constantly** -- `House Verticals`, `Window Verticals`/`Window Horizontals`, `Other Verticals`/`Horizontals`, `Snowflake Front Outlines`, a dozen+ `Snowflake Monster *` spoke/ring/arm groups, all resolve to submodel-qualified members and all get real effects (up to 328 placements on one group in Cold Heart). Two bugs were hiding this entirely:
- `load_show_models()` in `show.py` had a loop that explicitly `continue`d past every `<subModel>` child element -- `LightModel.submodels` was **always empty** for every model in the show. Fixed.
- `load_model_groups()`'s `resolve()` only accepted a group member if it matched a real top-level model name -- any `"Parent/Sub"` reference was treated as a stale/removed reference and silently dropped, which meant groups like `House Verticals` resolved to almost nothing and got excluded from the cast for being too small. Fixed to accept `Parent/Sub` when `Sub` is a real submodel of `Parent` (checked against the actual `<subModel name=...>` elements now captured above -- **not** just checking `Parent` exists, since some submodel references in this show's own groups turned out to be genuinely stale and should still be dropped, e.g. `Sun Room Star Left/Ring 1` doesn't actually exist on that model).
- Downstream, `generate_song.py`'s `build_cast()` needed `cat_of()`/position lookups to fall back from `"Parent/Sub"` to `Parent` for category and spatial-ordering purposes, since submodels aren't separately in the position table.

## Never target a *synthetic* group name directly -- broadcast to its real members instead

When no real xLights group covers a numbered family (e.g. the 5 real `Curtain Garage 1..5` panels, once the miswired `Curtain Garage` container is excluded -- see below), the fallback is prefix-stripping detection, same as when a show has zero groups at all. The stripped prefix (e.g. `"Curtain Garage"`) is **not a real xLights entity** -- placing an effect on it directly either orphans (renders nothing) or, worse, silently aliases onto an unrelated real model that happens to share the string. Any such synthetic cast entry must be marked and every effect placement routed through a broadcast helper (`add_target()`/`target_name()` in `generate_song.py`) that fans out to its real individual members instead of using the fake name. This bit twice in the same session: first the "Flood"/"Mini Tree Star"/"Snowflake" leftover buckets showed up as literal stale orphan elements, then fixing that surfaced that "Curtain Garage" (the excluded miswired model) was *also* colliding with a synthetic bucket of the same name and silently absorbing its effects.

## A model can look like the right container and still be wired wrong -- verify, don't just infer from shape

`Curtain Garage` has exactly the container signature (empty `Controller`, `NumStrings=3`) that correctly identifies `Curtain Front`/`North`/`South` as real spanning containers for their panels. But its `StartChannel` is `"!Front Curtain Center:1"` -- wired to the *front* curtain's controller, not the garage's -- and its `ScaleX` (19.6) is wildly smaller than the real 5-panel span (160). Effects placed on it land on the wrong physical curtain (or nowhere). This is a genuine data bug in the show file itself (Krists likely added `Curtain Garage 1..5` as real panels after `Curtain Garage` was first built, and never repointed or removed the old model) -- **worth mentioning to Krists directly, it's a 2-minute fix in the xLights GUI** (repoint or delete the model). Added to `MISWIRED_MODELS` in `generate_song.py` as a stopgap; the container-detection heuristic (empty Controller + NumStrings>1) is necessary but not sufficient -- cross-check `StartChannel`'s controller name against the model's own name when something is targeted but visibly doesn't light up.

## Cast pool size needs a hard cap independent of how many groups exist

Unlocking submodel groups roughly doubled the candidate pool from ~55 to 115+. Left uncapped, a whole song's worth of section-by-section rotation touches nearly the entire pool at least once, which pushed both total distinct targets and total placement count well past the human benchmark even with per-section *simultaneous* activity correctly capped -- rotation coverage over time is a separate axis from instantaneous density and needs its own cap. Fix: trim the pool to `TARGET_CAST_SIZE` (~62) after construction, always keeping containers/heroes/individual-fallback entries and a `MUST_HAVE_GROUPS` set of the major named whole-prop families (`Arches`, `C9s`, `Orbs`, `Floods`, `Tunnel`, `Candy Canes`, `Curtain Garage`...), taking a bounded weighted-random sample of the rest (biased toward larger groups). Two follow-on traps worth remembering if this gets touched again:
1. A must-have guarantee only works if the group actually got **constructed** as a cast entry in the first place -- must-haves need to claim members *before* the general smallest-first dedup pass runs, not just survive a later trim, or a smaller overlapping group (e.g. some `Horizontal C9s` variant) can starve out `C9s` before trimming ever sees it.
2. Sibling must-haves can partially overlap (`Orbs` is nested inside `Floods`'s raw member list) and leave a must-have below the usual "≥60% of its own membership" dedup threshold -- must-haves should accept any ≥2 leftover members rather than being held to that threshold, or they get dropped on a technicality.

## Absolute target-count ranges, not fractions of cast size

Per-section active-target count must be an **absolute** range tied to the measured human benchmark (mean simultaneous elements: 40-112), not `n_cast * fraction`. A fraction that looked right against a ~55-entry pool inflates simultaneous activity once the pool grows (e.g. from submodel groups) even though the fraction number is unchanged. Current bands in `generate_song.py`: energy `<0.30 -> 16`, `<0.50 -> 28`, `<0.70 -> 42`, `else -> 58` (feature-role count 1-3, +1 more on later repeats of a section type), always `min(n_cast, ...)`.

## Speed: short-and-frequent reads as rhythm, subdividing-a-block-faster reads as strobe

Direct correction from feedback, twice: round 3 concluded "scale chase rotations up with block duration" from one measurement and made arches blur; round 4's fix (subdividing motion blocks into half-bars during high-energy sections, "more happening = switch faster") produced the same complaint again ("really fast effects tend to look bad and strobey... I liked the C9s and verticals moving left/right, that wasn't too fast"). **The lesson holds across both attempts: don't increase a prop's own update rate to add energy.** Keep one motion effect change per full bar always, and get more "happening" during hot sections by lighting *more targets*, not by switching each one faster. Also: driving a group-level Chase effect via `Per Preview` buffer style on a visually complex prop (arches) produced results described as "almost random... not synced to anything... effects way off center" -- buffer-style semantics for group-level effects can't be verified without the xLights GUI, so for anything where that ambiguity risk matters, prefer explicit per-model cross-model-cascade placement (fully self-controlled timing, one member advancing per **beat**, alternating sweep direction) over trusting an inferred buffer style. This is exactly the "leaping arches" pattern from round 1's research, just re-confirmed as the safer default for scattered multi-member groups needing directional motion.

## Symmetric ensembles need a dedicated concept, and it's easy to half-implement

v8 round (2026-08-09), direct feedback: "the three front window matrices... often doing different things... let's keep them doing the same or at least symmetrical, I think in general symmetry is important" -- plus a *second*, subtler case of the same bug already live: the 5 garage curtain panels had identical ambient-layer timing (confirmed via diffing effect counts per layer: layers 0-2 were 17/21/83 on every panel) but layer 3 (signature cues) was uneven, 46 vs 23-27, because `arp_chase`/synth-stabs cycle through a family's members one-at-a-time to represent a *traveling* note or accent -- exactly right for `Arches`/`Orbs`/`Tunnel` (a sequence of positions is the point), exactly wrong for an ensemble meant to look uniform.

Built a `symmetric` flag (`SYMMETRY_FAMILIES` dict for physically-distinct-but-should-move-as-one models like the window matrix trio, auto-applied to every leftover-prefix synthetic family too since those are almost always panels of one physical surface) that gates every individual-member-cycling PASS 2 pool (`mel_pool`, `bass_pool`, `stab_pool`, `detail_pool`) -- symmetric targets are excluded from all of them, so they only ever get the uniform broadcast treatment.

**Caught myself half-implementing this once already**: defined `SYMMETRY_FAMILIES` and built it correctly in `build_cast()`, but forgot that `symmetric=True` groups still have `kind="group"` and therefore are still subject to the cast-size trim's random sampling -- since "Front Window Matrices" wasn't also listed by name in `MUST_HAVE_GROUPS`, one run's random trim silently dropped it entirely (zero effects, not just asymmetric ones -- worse than the bug being fixed). This is the exact same failure shape as the `C9s`/`Floods` must-have bug from the previous round, just recurring because two separate lists (`SYMMETRY_FAMILIES`, `MUST_HAVE_GROUPS`) had to independently agree on a name -- fixed by making the must-have filter check `t.get("symmetric")` automatically rather than relying on remembering to list a name twice. **General lesson: when a new "always keep this" category is added, make its guarantee structural (derived from a flag already on the object) rather than a second manually-synced name list** -- a name-matching guarantee is one missed entry away from silently doing nothing.

Residual known imperfection, not chased further given time: point-accent passes that pick ONE representative model to place a brief moment (`display_sweep` for riser sweeps) aren't filtered by the `symmetric` flag the way the cycling pools are, so a symmetric ensemble can occasionally get one extra hit on one member from a sweep event. Confirmed small in practice (341 vs 282 total effects across the window-matrix trio, vs. the 2x-scale asymmetry before the fix) but not eliminated.

## Not every visual bug is a design problem -- some are real xLights render errors, and file/parameter completeness matters

User's diagnostic instinct from experience: matrices flashing solid red usually means xLights is failing to render a referenced asset (missing image/video/shader) and showing an error state instead. None of this generator's effects reference external files, so the real culprit was narrower but the same *category* of problem: **forcing an unusual buffer-style/model combination that xLights doesn't actually support**. `curtain_close`/`curtain_open`/`ripple_implode`/`ripple_explode` hardcoded `Per Preview` (spanning-canvas) buffer style unconditionally -- correct and necessary when addressing a real multi-model xLights group, but applied indiscriminately it also landed on individual Custom-type models with irregular node layouts and no `NumStrings` at all (`Garage Roof`, `Tunnel Matrix Real`) where "span multiple models as one canvas" doesn't have a sensible meaning. Fixed by making buffer-style spanning conditional in `add_target()`: only inject `Per Preview` when the target is `kind=="group" and not synthetic` (i.e. actually a real xLights group with real members to span); every individual model gets `Default`, which is confirmed safe everywhere in real sequences. **Whenever an effect is being placed on both real groups and individual models through the same code path, treat any buffer-style override as something that needs to vary by target type, not a single hardcoded default.** Cannot fully confirm this was *the* red-flash cause without visual access to xLights -- flagged as the most plausible mechanism given the evidence, not a certainty.

## Diversity-aware sampling beats flat weighted sampling when candidates cluster around a few underlying things

The outline/submodel group pool (~24-37 candidates) is dominated by a couple of decorated props with many overlapping sub-variant groups (GE Grand Illusion and GE Rosa Grande alone contribute a dozen+ spoke/arm/ring groups). A flat weighted-random sample over that list reliably picked several GE Grand Illusion variants and left completely different props (plain snowflakes, stars) with zero representation -- confirmed by direct feedback ("the other snowflakes and stars don't seem to be using submodels"). Fixed by clustering candidates by their members' majority base model first, then round-robining one pick per cluster (shuffled cluster order, shuffled within each cluster) until the sample budget is used -- guarantees breadth across distinct decorated props instead of depth into whichever prop happens to have the most redundant group variants. General pattern: **when sampling from a candidate pool, check whether candidates cluster around a small number of "real" underlying things before doing a flat weighted sample** -- if they do, sample by cluster, not by raw candidate count, or a numerically-large cluster silently monopolizes the result.

## A quiet section's "story" can need its own detector, not just a lower activity fraction

Feedback across two rounds now: Gooey's opening ~60s is a repeating "big drop, slow fade" pattern (confirmed via direct RMS peak-picking: prominent hits at ~4.8s, 15.9s, 26.9s, 37.9s, 49.0s -- roughly every 11s), and generic low-activity ambient/pulse behavior there reads as "random flickering" rather than following that shape. The existing pre-drop-cutout detector only ever caught the FIRST of these, because it specifically requires near-silence (`<12% of peak`) immediately before the jump -- these decay to a moderate baseline (~40-50% of peak) between hits, never silence, so cutout detection structurally can't see hits 2-5. Needed a genuinely different, complementary detector (`detect_big_hits()`: `scipy.signal.find_peaks` on the RMS envelope with a height/prominence floor, restricted to sections below an energy ceiling so it doesn't fire redundantly during already-busy choruses) rather than retuning the existing one. Each hit gets a bright accent then an explicit long-fadeout wash across roughly half the cast (not the full cast -- that scale is reserved for the true pre-drop implosion moments, or the "big hit" stops being a smaller/different gesture by comparison). **When a described musical pattern doesn't match what an existing detector catches, check whether it's actually a structurally different signal (silence-then-jump vs. peak-with-decay-to-a-moderate-floor look completely different in the RMS envelope) before assuming the existing detector just needs different thresholds.**

Also: a fixed short duration (200ms) made a "somewhat slowly repeating" melodic run nearly invisible against surrounding activity. `arp_chase`'s per-note hold is now derived from the run's own onset spacing (median gap between notes, capped 220-900ms) so a slow melody actually reads as held notes rather than a flicker.

## Custom GLSL shaders: investigated, deliberately deferred

Direct ask (2026-08-09, on `Too Slow (All Night)`, round 6): "did you consider if you can make custom shaders... psychedelic patterns, textile/fabric patterns." xLights' `Shader` effect is real and heavily used in `Biometrix - Silent Night` (790 instances) -- it references an external `.fs` GLSL fragment shader file via `E_0FILEPICKERCTRL_IFS`, plus xLights-level uniform parameters (`uIntensity`, `uRotate`, `uZoom`, `uOffsetX/Y`, `Shader_Speed`) layered on top. **Deliberately did not attempt to author new `.fs` files**: Silent Night's own shader paths point at `/media/krists-auders/3162-3264/XLights 2024 copy/.../Shaders/*.fs` -- a different machine's old drive -- so even Krists's own reference shaders are likely broken on this Mac now, meaning there was no working local example to safely copy/adapt. No `.fs` files ship inside `/Applications/xLights.app`, and a web search turned up generic GLSL/Shadertoy info but nothing confirming xLights' specific dialect/uniform contract. Authoring GLSL blind against an unverified dialect risks creating exactly the render-error class of bug this whole thread has been fixing, not avoiding it. **Used `Plasma` instead** (native, no file reference, organic flowing texture, settings validated from a real sequence with zero ValueCurve complexity) as the safe partial substitute. **Revisit if**: real working `.fs` files turn up (from Krists's archives, or a downloadable xLights community shader pack) to use as a verified-safe template, or if `Pictures`/`Video` effects (referencing a PNG/video file I generate and fully control myself, lower risk than blind GLSL since only file-existence needs verifying, not an undocumented syntax) turn out to be a better fit for "textile/fabric pattern" specifically.

## Ambient layers can silently undercut a "blackout" moment

Direct feedback: pre-drop implosions "should go from almost no lights to almost all lights" but weren't dramatic enough. Root cause: the implosion's dark-hold placed an `Off` effect on layer 3 (signature), but layers 0-2 (ambient bed) kept their own independently-scheduled effects running underneath for that same window -- nothing in the generator ever suppressed them, so the "blackout" was never actually dark, just had an inert `Off` layer stacked on top of still-active ambient color. **A higher-numbered effect layer does not automatically override lower layers' visual output** (or at minimum, cannot be assumed to without visual verification) -- the actual fix has to be at the effect-placement level: `clip_for_blackout()` in `generate_song.py` post-processes the full effects list after both passes are built, truncating/splitting any layer 0-2 effect that overlaps a real blackout window (implosion's `[dark_start, drop]`) so nothing is scheduled there at all. Also varied the drop-hit itself by category (`drop_hit_key()`: Spirals/Pinwheel for large canvases, category-appropriate chase/ripple/wave elsewhere) instead of a flat `on_hit` everywhere, since "almost all lights on" also needs to look like *something*, not just brightness.

## A flash-rate ceiling is a real, useful constraint to enforce explicitly

Direct feedback: "anything faster than 2-4 flashes per second... is better done as a movement pattern... I don't like strobing." This is a concrete, checkable constraint the generator wasn't enforcing -- the PULSE role was thinning onsets by a fixed *index* stride (`every=1 or 2`), which doesn't bound the actual *time* gap between kept hits when onsets themselves are irregularly spaced (a dense hi-hat roll survives index-thinning just fine, arrives faster than 4Hz, reads as random). Fixed generally, not just for this one role: `split_flashes_and_bursts()` (min gap 0.28s, ~3.5Hz) separates isolated onsets (kept as discrete flashes) from runs of 3+ closely-spaced onsets (converted to ONE movement effect spanning the whole run instead of discrete hits) -- directly implementing "too fast for a flash -> should be a movement pattern" as a general-purpose primitive, not a one-off tweak. Applied to the PULSE role; not yet applied to the synth-stab/cymbal-detail passes (lower risk there since those already rotate across many different targets rather than hammering one, so dense clustering doesn't concentrate on a single prop the way drum-onset PULSE hits do) -- worth revisiting if strobing complaints persist on those specifically.

## A detector tuned for one melodic shape won't catch a structurally different one

Direct feedback: wanted a specific repeating string phrase in the chorus (described rhythm: 3 short notes, 1 long, 1 short, 2 long) represented as a side-to-side sway "like the house or dancers swaying" -- and it wasn't showing up at all. Neither existing melodic detector fits a sustained/legato line: the arpeggio detector requires a *short repeating cyclical* pitch pattern (period 2-8, same notes cycling), and the stab detector is tuned for *percussive* attacks (simultaneous amplitude + brightness jump). A legato string pad has irregular note *durations* without necessarily cycling on a short pitch period. Needed a third, structurally different detector: `detect_legato_phrase()` does onset+duration segmentation (each note's length = gap to the next onset) restricted to chorus-labeled sections, classifies short/long by a duration split, and drives alternating-direction `Wave` (safe, already-proven) across verticals/curtains/window-outline groups. **First attempt over-fired badly** (426 "notes" in one chorus, clearly picking up most of the mix's texture, not just a lead line) -- fixed by capturing onset *strength* at each onset and keeping only the most prominent ones when there are more candidates than a single repeating phrase plausibly needs (capped at 60, sorted by strength, not just take-everything). **General lesson: when asked to represent a specific musical element that doesn't match either of your existing detector shapes (cyclical-repeat vs. percussive-transient), build the third shape rather than forcing the closest existing one to fit** -- and expect a first pass at a new detector to over-fire; measure the raw candidate count before trusting it.

## Two rounds of "still some red flashes" narrowed the diagnosis further

Round 2 fix (buffer-style conditional) reduced but didn't eliminate red-flash reports, specifically still on Mega Tree and (to a lesser degree implied) elsewhere. Two more root causes found: (1) `feature_effect_for`'s large-canvas pool treated all "large" targets identically, but Mega Tree (`Tree` display type, 6 radial strings) is a fundamentally different shape from the flat/matrix-like canvases (Garage Roof, Sun Room Window Matrix, Tunnel Matrix, curtains) the pool was really validated against -- split into a narrower tree-specific pool (Spirals/Pinwheel only, the two effects with genuine community-standard tree usage) vs. the fuller flat-canvas pool; (2) the pre-drop implosion's collapse phase used `ripple_implode` **unconditionally on every cast member**, completely bypassing the category-aware effect selection in `feature_effect_for` -- Mega Tree was still getting Ripple through this second, separate code path even after the first fix. **When the same category of bug (an effect unsuited to a particular model shape) has more than one code path that can place it, fixing one path is not suffient -- grep for every place the suspect effect key is placed, not just the one you found first.** Confirmed via direct XML inspection post-fix: Mega Tree's Ripple instances are gone, replaced with Shockwave (the one effect proven safe across every round with zero red-flash reports against it).

## `argmax` over a lookahead window finds the loudest instant, not the drop onset

Direct feedback, very precisely diagnosed by the user: the final drop's dark-to-bright contrast looked exactly right, but landed "4 full beats late." Root cause in `detect_predrop_cutouts()` (`detect_cues.py`): after a quiet dip, it searched a 3-second lookahead window for where to place `drop_time` using `np.argmax()` -- the single loudest RMS instant *anywhere* in that window. If a beat a few positions later happens to hit marginally harder than the actual drop onset, `argmax` reports that later beat instead. Fixed by finding the FIRST index crossing the loud threshold (`np.argmax(look_norm >= loud_frac)`, which returns the first True) instead of the loudest instant. Verified precisely: the reported drop time for the case in question moved from 123.1s to 121.2s, a 1.87s shift -- **at 129.2 BPM that's 1.86s for 4 beats, matching "4 full beats late" almost exactly**. Also added deduplication of near-duplicate cutout candidates (merge within 3s, keep the higher-energy one) -- multiple brief quiet blips in a stuttering buildup were independently satisfying the detector and scheduling overlapping implosions, whose dark-hold windows compounded into an extended blackout. **When `argmax`/`argmin` is used to *locate an event* rather than just measure a peak value, check whether "the extreme value anywhere in a window" is actually the same thing as "the event you're trying to detect" -- they silently diverge whenever the true onset isn't also the single most extreme point nearby.**

## "Random feeling" flashes are a checkable property: proximity to the beat grid

Direct feedback: isolated flashes that "just briefly flash and it's not obvious why" and look glitchy, distinct from the earlier strobing complaint (this isn't about rate, it's about content with no audible rhythmic anchor at all). Built `near_beat_grid()`: a beat + half-beat position grid from `analysis.beats.beat_times`, and drop (not snap -- the user said "remove that") any synth-stab or cymbal-detail event further than ~90-120ms from the nearest grid point before it gets a flash. This is a general, reusable pattern for "does this moment have a legible rhythmic reason to exist" that's worth applying to any future one-off accent detector, not just these two.

## Prop SIZE changes which effect *types* are appropriate, independent of category

Direct feedback: Mega Tree and Tunnel Matrix Real "flash a single color" during the intro and it's "hard for the eyes" on something that large. The PULSE role (rhythmic flash-on-onset) was being assigned to `large`-canvas targets exactly like any small decorative prop, with no size-awareness at all -- a brief flash reads as a cute accent on a small prop and as an overwhelming wall-of-color event on a huge one. Fixed by redirecting `role == ROLE_PULSE and target.get("large")` to `ROLE_FEATURE` (real motion/pattern) instead of leaving it as a flash. **Category (what a prop *is*) and size (how much of the viewer's visual field it fills) are separate axes that both need to influence effect choice** -- this is the same underlying lesson as the tree-vs-flat-canvas buffer-style split, applied to a different dimension (rate/scale of visual change, not render compatibility).

## The generator architecture that matches this spec (`custom_tools/generate_song.py`)

Built 2026-08-09 as a ground-up rewrite once the above was measured. Shape to keep:

1. **`build_cast()`** (as of round 8): (a) container models first, excluding anything in `MISWIRED_MODELS`, (b) `HERO_MODELS` centerpiece props, (b2) auto-discovered submodel entries for any hero with 3+ real submodels (see "Submodel auto-discovery" below), (c) real xLights groups — `MUST_HAVE_GROUPS` (the major named families) claim their members before the general smallest-first dedup pass so a smaller overlapping group can't starve them out, (d) a prefix-fallback pass for leftover numbered families no real group covers (marked `synthetic=True`), (e) remaining individual leftovers. Then **trimmed** to `TARGET_CAST_SIZE` (70 as of round 8, was 62): keep containers/heroes/hero-submodels/individuals + all must-haves + a bounded weighted sample (`OUTLINE_SAMPLE_SIZE`, 16 as of round 8, was 12) of submodel/outline groups + a weighted-random sample of everything else. Without this cap, submodel groups roughly double the raw pool size and a whole song's rotation ends up touching nearly all of it. **Note**: hero-submodel entries are `kind="model"` (individual), so they land in the always-kept `keep` bucket unconditionally and bypass the group budget entirely — when adding a new always-kept category like this, `TARGET_CAST_SIZE` must be bumped by roughly how many new entries it adds, or it silently zeroes out the group-sampling budget for everything else (see "Submodel auto-discovery" below for the actual count).
2. **Never address a synthetic target by its (fake) name** — `add_target()`/`target_name()` broadcast to real individual members instead. See the dedicated section above; this bit twice.
3. **Orchestration pass**: per section, light an *absolute* target-count band by energy (16/28/42/58, not a fraction of cast size — see above), roles by rank — `feature` (motion) 38%, `pulse` 18%, `texture` 24%, rest `outline`, everything else fully OFF. Active set rotates by section index so different props lead, with `t.get("large")` also treated as "prominent" (near-guaranteed active) alongside big groups (round 10 — see below, hero MODELS have `n==1` and were losing the rotation lottery for 20-30s stretches). One motion-effect change per full bar, never faster, even in hot sections (see speed section above) — extra energy comes from lighting more targets, not switching faster.
4. **`wants_motion(target)`** (round 11): `large` OR `cat=="arch"` OR (`cat=="custom"` AND `n>=4`) — any target this is true for never gets a flat flash/wash/twinkle from ANY pass (PULSE role, TEXTURE/OUTLINE role fallback, four-on-the-floor anchor selection, `stab_pool`, `detail_pool`, `display_sweep`'s target lists). Started as a `large`-only exclusion, generalized once Candy Canes (`custom`, `n=8`) turned out to need the identical treatment through five separate call sites.
5. **Arch-category targets get a dedicated beat-paced cross-model cascade** instead of a group-level Chase effect (see above) — sorted by X position, one member advances per beat, alternating sweep direction, `on_hit` (sharp ~20ms attack, not `on_swell`'s soft 150ms fade — see round 8 below) with a 260-550ms hold (never faster).
6. **Outline-shaped groups get `single_line` treatment**: any group whose name matches `OUTLINE_NAME_HINTS` (vertical/horizontal/outline/spoke/arm/petal) is force-categorized as `single_line` regardless of its parent model's real category, so window outlines and snowflake spokes get the same chase/wave treatment as the C9s that's already confirmed to work well.
7. **Section-instance escalation**: count each label's occurrence; later instances get more feature targets and bolder effect pools, so repeats don't look identical.
8. **`LARGE_CANVAS`/`wants_motion` targets get a richer effect pool** (`SAFE_LARGE`, module-level constant: Spirals/Pinwheel/Plasma/real GLSL `Shader`/Chase/Wave) instead of washes, for FEATURE, TEXTURE, and OUTLINE roles alike (round 11 — TEXTURE/OUTLINE were missed in round 10, causing the "plain green, not impactful" drop complaint: a flat Color Wash/Shimmer spanning a whole 15-30s section buried the brief patterned drop-hit underneath it). `Curtain` is reserved only for genuine curtain-shaped containers.
9. **One shared shader/pattern choice per section for cohesion** (round 11): `shared_large_key = rng.choice(SAFE_LARGE)`, computed once per section and passed to every `feature_effect_for()` call for a `large` target that section, so simultaneously-visible large canvases read as one coordinated look rather than independent animations. NOT used for the climax drop hit itself (see below) — that's deliberately the one moment where different canvases showing different things reads as rich rather than disjointed.
10. **Song-level palette family + dedicated climax palette** (round 11): `song_palette_cycle` is a fixed, per-song-random subset of 4 from `PAL_CYCLE` (was: cycle through all 7 every section, which read as disjointed) — real variety across sections, real cohesion within a song. `climax_sections` (the section containing the highest-`drop_energy` implosion, or the single highest-energy section) gets a dedicated `"climax"` palette (`#FFFFFF/#FFD700/#0047FF`) instead of the ordinary rotation, and `drop_hit_key(t, is_climax=True)` draws large-canvas drop patterns from the FULL shader-inclusive pool independently per target (richness) rather than the plain pinwheel/spirals binary ordinary drops get.
11. **Signature cues on a dedicated top layer**: fast upward synth zips → whole-display bottom-to-top sweep; long risers → directional builds; synth stabs → fast pops on rotating props; strongest pre-drop cutouts only (top 6 by drop energy) → implode + explicit `Off` hold + full-display hit; arpeggios → chase across a prop family; cymbal detail on a rotating decorative target. The implosion collapse effect itself is `shockwave` for any `large` target, `ripple_implode` otherwise.
12. **Layer discipline**: 0 = outline/texture, 1 = feature motion, 2 = pulse, 3 = signature cues.
13. **Four-on-the-floor persistent pulse** (round 9): when `detect_four_on_floor()` finds a kick on ≥80% of active beats, a quarter-note `on_pop` pulse runs on ~8 anchor props (excluding `wants_motion` targets) spread across the facade through every section above 0.45 energy.
14. **String-sway as a facade-wide pendulum** (round 9/10): `detect_legato_phrase()`'s short/long note classification drives a zone-index pendulum across X-sorted verticals/curtains — long notes continue the current direction, short notes reverse it, matching a real described riff rhythm rather than a fixed per-note-index pendulum.

## ROUND 25 (2026-08-10): per-prop measurement built -- and it proved the movement was working all along

### The channel map is DISCOVERED, not computed

Everything per-prop needs to know which fseq channels belong to which model. Computing it from `xlights_networks.xml` (sum each controller's `MaxChannels`, resolve `StartChannel="!Controller:offset"`) **does not work** -- it yields 128,361 channels against the fseq's actual 153,230, a 24,869 gap from allocation details not worth reverse-engineering, and any error would silently corrupt every measurement built on it.

`custom_tools/channel_map.py` instead **asks xLights**: it builds a probe sequence that BINARY-ENCODES each model's index (in time slot `f`, every model whose index has bit `f` set is lit white), renders it with the real engine, and decodes each channel's on/off pattern across 12 slots. Result: **227 of 235 models mapped, 0 undecodable channels**, exact by construction, self-validating. Cached to `.generation/channel_map.json`; probe files are deleted after.

Incidental finding: models with `Active="0"` DO light in the probe when effects are placed directly on them, so xLights renders them into the fseq. Our exclusion of them stands (it matches Krists's stated preference and stops voices being wasted on hidden props) but the reason is "not physically present", not "renders nothing".

### `prop_probe.py`: the lit-node centroid answers "is this prop moving, and which way"

Within a model, channels run in node order along the strand, so the mean index of the lit nodes is a POSITION along the prop. Watching it over frames distinguishes travelling / oscillating / static -- and on a vertically-mounted strip, strand order IS physical height, so vertical movement is measurable **without any 3D geometry**, which is exactly the part of a visual preview that cannot be made accurate.

### The measurement's first real use nearly caused a WRONG fix -- twice

1. First run reported `Vertical Strip Front Left` as **100% lit with the centroid frozen at exactly 50.0%** -- apparently static. Inspecting the .xsq showed the chase present and correct on layer 1, with a 27-second `Twinkle` on layer 0 underneath. Suppressed that ambient fill for any prop carrying movement... and the output was **byte-identical**. Re-probing caught it: ensemble props draw the PULSE role and fell through to a SECOND `twinkle_soft` placement in that branch. Fixed there too.
2. Still 100% lit. Before changing anything else, questioned the metric -- and it was wrong. The whole-house shader bed puts a DIM wash on every node, so an absolute "lit" threshold (sum > 40 of 765) counted everything as lit and pinned the centroid at the midpoint. Re-measuring against **each frame's own peak** (nodes above 60% of frame max) showed the truth: the bright spot travels 0% -> 28% -> 46%, covering **96% of the prop**, oscillating. **The vertical movement has been rendering correctly the whole time.**

The actual problem is CONTRAST, not motion: peak ~176 against a base wash of ~77 is roughly 2.3:1, so the movement is present but muted. One of the two wash layers is now removed; the whole-house shader bed is the other, and that's a deliberate trade for Krists to weigh since he likes the bed.

**Lessons, both expensive:**
- **A measurement can invent a bug.** Two fixes were made against a metric that was itself wrong; the second produced byte-identical output, which is the signal that should have prompted questioning the metric sooner. When a fix changes nothing measurable, suspect the measurement before adding another fix.
- **Absolute thresholds lie under a full-coverage layer.** Anything asking "which part of this prop is lit" must measure relative to the frame's own dynamic range, because a dim wash over everything makes an absolute test useless.

All four regenerated clean: Gooey v19 (0.2%/1.6% red, 43% lit), Too Slow v19 (0.1%/0.9%, 36%), WhatsApp v14 (0.2%/1.0%, 41%), 12 to 12 v13 (1.3%/11.9%, 45%).

## ROUND 24 (2026-08-10): harmony and vocals wired in -- for BOUNDARIES and TIMING, not for colour or words

Two new analysis inputs, scoped deliberately narrowly after measuring what each is actually good for. Krists agreed the scoping before wiring: *"chords are good for changes, not overall feel. And for vocals use them more for timing or energy."*

**Chord CHANGES as pattern-change boundaries** (`change_marks()`). Large-canvas patterns and the whole-house bed used to change every N bars -- correct musically but arbitrary. They now prefer bar boundaries that coincide with a real chord change, so the change feels motivated. Falls back to the fixed cadence when a song hasn't enough harmonic movement, guarded by BOTH a floor and a ceiling on how many chord-aligned marks a section yields (too few = the canvas sits static for ages; too many = it out-paces the cadence it replaced). This graceful degradation matters more than it sounds: measured chord richness varies enormously by genre -- WhatsApp 82 segments / 78 changes, 12 to 12 91/86, but Gooey has one 54-SECOND D:maj block. Both outcomes are correct for their song.

**Key/mode deliberately NOT used for colour**, despite being the obvious idea and despite detecting reliably (0.89-0.95 confidence on three of four songs). All four songs measured so far are MINOR, so a mode-driven palette family would have *reduced* cross-song variety versus the existing random-family pick. Worth re-testing if the library ever gains major-key songs. **Lesson: a detector working well is not sufficient reason to wire it in -- check what the distribution of its output across the real corpus actually buys you.**

**Vocals used for timing and energy only.** Word IDENTITY is unreliable on sung material (mean confidence 0.62; a first hook attempt returned the pronoun "her"), so nothing depends on what is sung:
- `vocal_fraction()` feeds section headroom as a third energy input at 15% weight alongside instrument count (45%) and loudness (40%) -- a section where the singer is actually singing reads as fuller than an instrumental passage at the same loudness.
- Phrase STARTS place a small swell on whichever prop already carries the `vocal` voice. Sparse by nature (18-45 per song) so it can't become flicker, and on a small prop by construction since `VOICE_PROP_PREFS` never puts `vocal` on a large canvas -- matching "use smaller props for following melodies or vocals."

Both cache to `.generation/<Song>/` (`harmony.json`, `lyrics.json`); `lyrics.py` caches ONLY the Whisper transcription and recomputes derived hook/phrases each call, so those heuristics can be re-tuned without re-running Whisper. It also skips Whisper entirely on a near-silent vocals stem (an instrumental's stem exists but is empty -- file presence proves nothing).

All four regenerated and rendered clean: Gooey v18 (0.2%/1.6% red, 43% lit), Too Slow v18 (0.1%/0.9%, 36%), WhatsApp v13 (0.2%/1.0%, 40%), 12 to 12 v12 (1.3%/11.9%, 44%).

**On the previewer question** (asked, answered, not built): xLights already does the rendering -- `-r` writes the `.fseq`, which holds the true per-channel output, so a previewer would REPLAY xLights' own numbers rather than simulate its effect engine. Colour/brightness would be exact. Only GEOMETRY is approximate, and the mapping is derivable (`StartChannel` is controller-relative, `xlights_networks.xml` maps controllers to absolute channels) -- but 16 models carry non-trivial 3D rotation (Tunnel Matrix `RotateX=90`, Garage Roof `-68.9`) and custom models encode node layout in a `CustomModel` string, so a flat 2D plot would misplace exactly the props most worth checking. The cheap high-value half is a per-prop TIME-SERIES check ("did this prop's pixels move vertically in this window?"), which needs only channel->model mapping; the picture-of-the-house half needs the 3D work.

## ROUND 22 (2026-08-10): the white matrices were MY jitter clamping to degenerate values

### White shaders: sampling around a near-edge default and then CLAMPING piles probability on the boundary

Reported: *"one of the shaders might not be working, multiple songs have sections where multiple matrices are just white. Similar to how they were when they were red but these are white."* Found by extending the fseq analysis with a near-white measure (min channel > 170 and max-min < 40) -- WhatsApp had **70 sampled frames above 35% white, in a continuous run from 41s**, which mapped exactly to `Wisps.fs` on Garage Roof + Sun Room Window Matrix over 40.7-55.7s.

Cause was the round-15 parameter jitter, not the shader. It sampled `uniform(default - spread, default + spread)` and then **clamped** to `[MIN, MAX]`. Wisps' defaults sit near their range edges (`glow=-6` of `[-40,0]`, `zoom=11` of `[0,100]`, `amp=0.1` of `[0,1]`), so clamping piled probability onto the exact boundary. Real songs came out with `zoom=0`, `amp=0`, and on Gooey `glow=0` -- maximum glow with zero wave amplitude, i.e. a blown-out white wash. Fixed by sampling INSIDE a 6%-of-span margin instead of clamping to the edges: an extreme is now merely possible rather than a probability spike, and no parameter can land on a degenerate endpoint. Result: WhatsApp 70 -> 2 white frames, Gooey 38 -> 13 (and the 13 are intentional -- they align with big-hit/implosion white accents and the softened pre-drop blackout, where outlines-only-on-white legitimately makes ~100% of a *tiny* lit set white).

**Lesson: `clamp(jitter(x))` is not a safe way to randomise a bounded parameter.** Whenever the nominal value sits near a bound, clamping converts the tail into a spike exactly at the worst value. Sample within an inset range instead. Also worth noting the shape of this bug: a feature added to increase VARIETY (round 15) silently created a systematic DEFECT, and it took two rounds and a new measurement axis to see it.

### Shader speed: vary downward, not symmetrically

*"There are a few shaders that are ironically too fast, some on the matrices and some on the whole house."* The risk is asymmetric -- too slow still reads as texture, too fast reads as strobing on a big prop -- so the jitter range should be too. Speed jitter 0.55-1.6x -> **0.45-1.05x**, and the whole-house bed multiplier 0.22 -> **0.12** (its apparent speed at any single prop is far higher than the same value inside one matrix, and this is the second reduction).

### Direction changes keyed to BEAT NUMBER, not to a per-hit counter

*"The vertical and horizontal movements seem to change directions on somewhat random seeming beats... rhythmic movements like that should probably be mostly on quarter notes or maybe 8th notes at the fastest."* Direction had been alternating on each prop's own hit index, so two props with different hit counts drift out of phase and the house stops swaying together. `beat_phase(t, every=2)` derives direction from the beat index, so every prop reverses at the same musical instant (half-notes -- flipping every quarter reads as agitation rather than sway).

### The ensemble was driven by HARMONY; it should be driven by the DRUM

`ensemble_voice` preference was `chord > snare > kick` -- the most melodic option first. A chord line lands wherever the harmony moves, frequently off the felt pulse, which is literally the "random seeming beats" report. Now `kick > snare > chord`.

### Emphasise whichever voice actually dominates THIS song

*"In Gooey in particular I want more focus on the bass... Not sure how to generalize that... somehow we need to identify if it's bass heavy then let's emphasize that or if another voice is very prominent."* Added a prominence score (onset count x mean onset strength) over the rhythm-driver voices (kick/snare/bass/chord); the top two each get up to 2 EXTRA props performing the same voice in unison -- *"it's okay if multiple props all follow the same beat, if anything that makes it more clear and rhythmic and dramatic."* Extras go into `prop_voice`, so they're both reserved from the generic passes and perform the voice, with no other code changes. Measured: Gooey elects **bass** (adds Icicles + GE Grand Illusion SOL Outer Rings) -- exactly the emphasis asked for, arrived at from measurement rather than hardcoding.

Renders: Gooey v16 (0.3%/1.6% red, 43% lit), Too Slow v16 (0.1%/0.9%, 37%), WhatsApp v11 (0.2%/1.0%, 41%), 12 to 12 v10 (1.3%/12.2% red, 44%) -- 12 to 12's red rose from 0.2%/1.7% as the new jitter reshuffled shader parameters; still comfortably inside the human band (mean 0.3-11%) but the one number that moved the wrong way this round.

## ROUND 21 (2026-08-10): the vertical-movement answer was in v5 all along -- and two real beat-grid bugs

### Vertical props are 1D STRANDS. Chase along them; `Bars(direction=up)` does nothing.

Third failed attempt at vertical movement finally solved by taking Krists's own hint -- *"look back at like v5 or something a while back, we had great movement on all the window outlines and verticals."* Opening `Gooey (v5).xsq` answered it in one step: it drove each **`Vertical Strip *` MODEL** with `SingleStrand` + **`Per Model Single Line`** + "Bounce from Left", 253 times each.

Why every previous attempt failed: those props are `DisplayAs="Single Line"` — **1D strands**. A `Bars` effect needs a 2D buffer for "up" to mean anything, so `direction=up` on a 1D strand degenerates and produces no vertical travel at all. The effect key was literally named `sweep_up`/`bars_up_in` and still moved nothing vertically. A chase ALONG the strand, by contrast, physically *is* up-and-down, because the strand is mounted vertically. Added `strand_up`/`strand_down`/`strand_bounce` (SingleStrand + `Per Model Single Line`) and pointed `line_motion_for` at them. Verified: Corner/Other/Right/Left Verticals and Icicles all now on `Per Model Single Line` chases.

**Lesson, and it cost three rounds: when a prop's motion won't work, check its `DisplayAs` before choosing an effect. A 1D strand, a 2D matrix and a cone need genuinely different effect families, and an effect whose NAME says "up" will silently do nothing on a buffer with no vertical axis.** Also: **when the user says an old version did it right, go read that version** — it was a two-minute lookup that beat three rounds of reasoning about geometry.

### The downbeat grid disagrees with the beat grid by 26-62ms

`downbeat_times` and `beat_times` come from different estimators and don't perfectly agree. Everything bar-aligned (shader/pattern changes, feature motion) sits on downbeats while everything pulse-aligned (kick pulse, voice hits) sits on beats/onsets — so that ~40ms shows up as the two families never quite landing together. Direct report: *"the shaders often are not on beat."* Now snapped onto the beat grid.

**Guarded, after the first attempt produced a 21-SECOND correction**: the two grids don't cover identical spans, so some downbeats have no detected beat anywhere near them, and naive nearest-match would have stacked many bar boundaries onto one instant. Only snap within a quarter-beat; otherwise keep the original. Result: 82/87 (Gooey) and 107/123 (WhatsApp) snapped, max correction ~160ms. **Lesson: a "snap to nearest" over two independently-derived grids needs a distance ceiling, or it silently teleports outliers.**

### The whole-house shader bed was starting before the kick

Measured on The WhatsApp Shakespeare: the bed began at **6.97s while the kick enters at 8.82s** — 1.85s of full-house shader already running before the moment that's meant to be the song kicking into gear. *"The shaders suddenly come on the wrong beat too early, and makes the main beat drop when the kick drum kicks in less impactful."* The bed is the largest single visual element in the show, so it arriving early is the thing most able to spoil that entrance. Now clamped to start no earlier than `first_kick_time`. Verified: moved 6.97s → 8.85s, 1ms off the nearest beat.

All four regenerated clean: Gooey v15 (0.3%/1.8% red, 43% lit), Too Slow v15 (0.1%/0.9%, 38%), The WhatsApp Shakespeare v10 (0.2%/1.4%, 47%), 12 to 12 v9 (0.2%/1.7%, 44%).

## ROUND 20 (2026-08-10): "still only side to side" -- a baked-in buffer style, and a pass that ignored the fix

Round 19 nominally gave vertical props `sweep_up`/`sweep_down`, and the report came back: *"Still not seeing any up down movement at all on verticals or outlines. Only side to side."* Two independent causes, both worth remembering:

**1. `_bars()` had `PERPROP` ("As Pixel") baked into its defaults.** As Pixel makes each MODEL a single pixel, so `direction=up` on a group of vertical strips travels ACROSS the strips -- which is side-to-side motion, exactly what was reported, from an effect whose name and direction parameter both say "up". Added `bars_up_in`/`bars_down_in` using `PERMODEL` so the bar travels up/down INSIDE each strip. **Lesson: a helper's default buffer style silently determines the AXIS the effect moves along. Naming an effect `sweep_up` does not make it move up.**

**2. The unison ensemble hardcoded `chase_dots_lr/rl` for every member regardless of orientation** -- and since that pass is the main source of movement on verticals and outlines (1500-3000 placements/song), the orientation logic added in round 19 (`line_motion_for`, living in the voice path) essentially never applied to them. Now each ensemble member moves along its own axis while still firing on the same beat in the same phase, so the unison reads as one gesture without forcing one shared direction. **Lesson: when a fix targets one code path, check which path actually produces the majority of the behaviour being complained about -- verified here by counting placements per pass, not by reading.**

Verified in the .xsq afterwards: Corner/Other/Right Verticals and Icicles all on `Bars up/down` with `Per Model Default`; C9s correctly retain horizontal `Left-Right`/`Right-Left` chases (a gutter run should move along its length). Ensemble slots now go to the real props (Icicles, C9s, Corner/Right/Left/Other Verticals, Other Horizontals) with the GE sub-detail families ranked below them.

All four regenerated clean: Gooey v14 (0.3%/1.8% red, 44% lit), Too Slow v14 (0.1%/0.9%, 39%), The WhatsApp Shakespeare v9 (0.2%/1.4%, 49%), 12 to 12 v8 (0.2%/1.6%, 45%).

Chasing "the stars still only have left/right group effects, I still don't see any per-star effects" led to a much larger finding: **`xlights_rgbeffects.xml` marks 123 models `Active="0"`, and `build_cast()` had never looked at that attribute.** Effects on those models render nothing. - The `Tunnel Arches 1..80` preview models are in this set too -- which is the same fact earlier rounds had hand-coded as a special case, without realising the layout already stated it declaratively.

Krists confirmed the principle immediately: *"I see you were sequencing disabled props, we should only sequence enabled props so we don't lose important voices to props that are hidden."*

**But `Active="0"` means two different things, and filtering naively would have broken working behaviour.** Measured before enabling:

- **WITHOUT a `Controller` (only 3: `Curtain Front/North/South`)** -- a VIRTUAL CONTAINER whose child panels do the output. These must STILL be sequenced: Silent Night puts 196 effects on each. Filtering on `Active` alone would have destroyed the container-vs-panel curtain targeting that several earlier rounds were spent getting right.

Also verified no cast group is emptied by the rule before shipping it (Candy Canes keeps 4 of 8 live members, Stars 10 of 12, C9s 7 of 8). **Lesson: a layout attribute can encode facts the generator has been re-deriving by hand for months -- but confirm what it means for each SHAPE of model before acting on it, and check nothing you depend on disappears.** Applied at all four cast entry points; the catch-all "anything still uncovered, individually" step was the one still leaking disabled models after the first three were fixed.

### Per-star effects: stop arguing with the buffer style, just write per-model effects

`Per Model Default` was being written correctly (verified 256/256 effects on the Stars group) and is a valid style used 150x in Krists's own sequences -- yet the reported result was still group-level. Rather than keep debating it, added `BROADCAST_GROUPS`: effects are written onto each MEMBER MODEL individually with a 60ms stagger. The .xsq now literally contains elements named `Star 3 24in` with 108 effects each and **zero** on the `Stars` group element. Unambiguous, and verifiable without needing to see it render. (The stars Krists means are `Star 1..4` in 12in/24in -- all active; the similarly-named bare `Star 1`/`Star 2`/`Megatree Star` are the disabled ones.)

### Tunnel needs `Vertical Stack` for built-in effects -- confirmed from his own sequences

*"When applying cone effects to the tunnel matrix, I think we need to change the render mode to stacked or something like that... otherwise you just see lines instead of the true pattern. The shaders etc work fine but not many of the built in effects."* Exactly right, and confirmed rather than guessed: Pink Pony Club drives the Tunnel with Morph/Wave/Spirals/On/Color Wash **all on `Vertical Stack`** (69/64/30/20/9 uses). `Tunnel Matrix Real` is a Custom model with 12 strings; the default buffer lays them end-to-end so a 2D pattern renders as smeared lines. Shaders are exempt -- they render their own coordinate space -- which matches his observation precisely.

### Orientation and length now drive linear-prop motion (`line_motion_for`)

Three corrections in one helper:
- **Orientation**: *"I don't see any vertical movement chases on the verticals... I think I might see left/right movement across verticals which just makes them strobe randomly."* A group of vertical strips spanned as one canvas can only chase HORIZONTALLY across the strips, and since each strip lights whole, that reads as sequential flashing. Vertical-named groups now get per-model Bars travelling up/down INSIDE each strip.
- **Length**: dot count scales with the run's physical width -- 4 dots for long gutter runs, 2 for short window outlines. *"The chases on the window outlines and verticals etc don't need that many because they're smaller."*
- **Speed**: *"The c9 chases are too fast too."* Long runs now start a new travel every OTHER hit, doubling each chase's crossing time.

### Loudness: rank-normalise, don't divide by the peak

*"The intensity or loudness measurement still seems not sensitive enough, mild drops in energy should still have some effect but it seems to only pick up large changes."* Precisely what peak-relative ratios do wrong on compressed modern masters: a clearly audible dip might only move RMS from 0.92 to 0.83 of peak, and the mapping barely registers it. Now each section is ranked against the song's OWN distribution (quietest ~0, loudest ~1 whatever the absolute spread), blended 70/30 with the peak ratio so a genuinely uniform song doesn't get swings invented from noise.

### The shader bed now follows the same signal as everything else

*"The shaders somehow don't always seem to follow the intensity changes, they often are on when the other lights go off."* Root cause: the whole-house bed still gated on the coarse `section.energy_level` while the activity budget had moved to the continuous loudness measure -- so the two could straightforwardly disagree, and the bed staying lit through a quiet passage is exactly that disagreement. Both now read `section_loudness`.

Also this round: a stray `import re` INSIDE `build_cast()` shadowed the module-level import and broke the new Active lookup with `UnboundLocalError` -- worth remembering that a redundant local import is not harmless.

## ROUND 18 (2026-08-10): buffer style is a CREATIVE choice, and loudness beats voice-count for dynamics

Seven items. Several turned out to be the same underlying mistake: using a group-SPANNING buffer style on a group whose members are physically separate objects.

**`PERMODEL` ("Per Model Default") introduced, and it fixes four complaints at once.** Until now the generator only had two buffer styles in play: `SPAN`/Per Preview (whole group as one canvas) and `PERPROP`/As Pixel (each model = one pixel, for cross-prop chases). Both are *spanning* styles -- they express motion by lighting members in sequence. For a group of DISCRETE SCATTERED OBJECTS that is the wrong idiom entirely, and produces strobing:
- **Stars**: "they have group effects that don't look good, they should mostly have effects that render per prop, otherwise the left right across the whole group of stars just looks like flashing and strobing." A chase across 12 physically separated stars can only light them one after another; at rhythm speed that IS strobing. Now Per Model (verified: 256/256 effects), so each star animates inside its own geometry.
- **Mini trees**: "I notice it's always side to side, we should do up and down too." Root cause was structural, not a bad effect choice -- `Mini Trees` is a 2-MEMBER group, so any group-spanning chase can only chase *between the two trees*, which is literally side-to-side. Per Model plus `sweep_up`/`sweep_down` (Bars) gives each tree vertical travel up its own cone. Verified: 142 Bars effects.
- **Floods**: see below.
General rule now encoded in `PER_MODEL_GROUPS`, plus automatically for any non-large `tree`-category group. **Lesson: buffer style isn't just a render-correctness setting (which is how it was treated for many rounds, after the curtain-spanning bug) -- it determines whether a group reads as ONE object or as MANY, and that's a creative decision that has to match the prop's physical reality.**

**Floods are a wash light, not a rhythm prop** (`SLOW_PROPS`). "The floodlights are too stroby. I like how the orbs flash and stuff because they're all together and it looks great when they sort of sparkle, but with the floods doing that it's just strobing... they can add drama when we cut out a lot of lights because they light up the whole house." Precisely observed: a flood aims at the whole house, so any hit from it changes the brightness of EVERYTHING -- the same sparkle that's charming on a cluster of small orbs is the entire scene blinking. Floods now thin to at most one hit per two beats regardless of what their assigned instrument does, and get a long `wash` bloom instead of a hit.

**Long lines want multiple dots, not one fast dot** (`_chase(chases=...)`). "It should move side to side but not across the entire line because it's a long line and it just doesn't look good going that fast. Multiple spots or dots or lines can all move side to side instead of a single one going all the way across." With N dots evenly spaced on the strand, each only travels 1/N of the line before handing over, so the same musical timing yields a much calmer apparent speed. `chase_dots_lr/rl` (4 chases) now drives all `single_line` voice motion and the unison ensemble. Verified on C9s: 151 four-dot vs 13 single-dot.

**The unison ensemble was excluding the exact props it was for.** "I don't see any of the movement in the window and matrix outline verticals and horizontals like we had before." The pool took `[:10]` in raw cast order, and the first ten single_line/icicle entries were four single-member `Sun Room Window Matrix/*` submodels (n=1 -- they cannot chase at all) plus the big GE decorated-prop spoke families. The real `Window Verticals`, `Corner Verticals`, `Other Verticals`, `Right Verticals` sat at indices 11-21 and never made the cut. Replaced with an explicit ranking (name matches vertical/horizontal/outline/c9/icicle first, sub-detail families last, then by size) and an `n >= 2` floor. Verified restored: Corner Verticals 241 effects, Other Verticals 279, Right Verticals 277, Window Verticals 236. **Lesson, third time this shape has appeared: any `[:N]` cap over an unsorted pool is a silent selection policy. Sort by what you actually want before truncating.**

**Loudness, not just voice count, drives dynamics.** Direct and correct critique of last round's headroom feature: "the number of voices isn't quite enough of a measurement since often the voices are all there but more mellow for a second and then the bass comes in really hard and that should be a bigger visual moment." An arrangement can keep every instrument present while dropping a long way in intensity -- voice counting sees no change at all. Added per-section RMS loudness as a continuous 0-1 ratio of the song's loudest section, measured from the audio rather than reusing `section.energy_level` (which is already normalised and quantised into the coarse activity bands, so it can't express "same instruments, more mellow"). Combined 50/50 with the voice ratio; headroom range widened from 0.65-1.0 to 0.5-1.0. Measured: Gooey has 10/19 sections below 60% of its peak loudness -- exactly the dynamic range that was being flattened.

**Drops graded by measured strength, and fills now dip.** Alternating full/soft blackouts gave only two levels and ignored drop size, so a modest drop could go pitch black while a bigger one didn't. Now ranked by `drop_energy`: strongest third go fully dark, rest keep outlines. Plus, activity drops to 75% across any section ending in a drum fill -- "dip the lit percentage when there is a fill since that's usually before a new section and the new section start should feel impactful."

All four regenerated and rendered clean, lit fraction at human level: Gooey v12 (0.5%/2.1% red, 42% lit), Too Slow v12 (0.2%/1.7%, 49%), The WhatsApp Shakespeare v7 (0.2%/1.5%, 53%), 12 to 12 v6 (0.4%/2.0%, 49%).

## ROUND 17 (2026-08-10): rhythm as MOVEMENT, and two more "prop never entered the cast" bugs

Follow-up to the voice system, which was confirmed working ("the voice detection and rhythm is working really well"). Nine items, all addressed.

**Rhythm carried by movement, not flashes** (`VOICE_MOTION_BY_CAT`). "Movement can also be rhythmic. Like when the mini trees flash to the kick drum, they could also have an up down movement to the rhythm." Each voice hit now places an effect that SPANS to the next hit (capped at a bar) rather than a brief flash, with direction alternating per hit so the prop rocks instead of restarting. Motion is chosen by prop CATEGORY, not by instrument, because what "moving in rhythm" means is a property of shape: `tree` -> meteors down / chase from middle (travel along the cone axis), `single_line` -> chase L/R (run along its length), `icicle` -> wave, `matrix` -> chase from/to middle. Measured on Gooey: Mini Trees went from flash-dominated to Meteors x74 + SingleStrand x68; C9s to SingleStrand x164.

**Speed limit on movement** (`VOICE_MOTION_MIN_GAP_S = 0.22`): "they're too random and stroby on anything faster" than quarters/eighths. Below that gap a moving effect can't complete enough travel to read as movement -- it smears. Faster voices (hats) keep a short flash, which at that rate reads as shimmer/texture, which is the correct idiom for hats anyway.

**Unison line ensemble** -- deliberately the OPPOSITE of the voice system, and both are correct. "Having many of the verticals or horizontals or c9s or window outlines doing the SAME rhythmic movement can be super effective visually." Voice assignment is one-prop-one-instrument, for attribution; this is many-props-one-instrument moving identically, for scale. Driven by the slowest available voice (chord > snare > kick) with an explicit quarter-note floor, because it fires on up to 10 props simultaneously and is therefore the most conspicuous possible place to get the rate wrong. ~1500-3000 placements per song.

**Submodels allowed as voice carriers, one rhythm per physical prop.** "Using submodels can still work as long as we don't use multiple submodels on the same prop for different rhythms." Prominence for sub-details went 0 -> 1 (usable but outranked by nameable props), and picking any target now also claims its `_base_model()` and every member's base model, so a second voice can never land on a sibling submodel of the same object.

**Ranking bug caught while doing that**: the assignment loop `break`ed at the first preferred category with ANY candidate, so once submodels became eligible it handed the chords to "GE Grand Illusion Spokes GRP" (50-member sub-detail that happens to be `single_line`) instead of falling through one category to Orbs. Fixed to rank across all preferred categories at once with prominence outranking category order. **Lesson: relaxing an eligibility gate can expose a latent ordering bug that the stricter gate was accidentally hiding -- re-check selection quality after loosening any filter, don't just check that the newly-allowed things can now be chosen.**

**Two more "prop never entered the cast" bugs, same shape as Mini Trees in round 12.** "We're not really utilizing the stars very much, they just have the whole house effects." Dumping `build_cast()` showed the layout has a real 12-member `Stars` group and a 15-member `House Stars`, and NEITHER reached the cast -- only four tiny fragments (Megatree Star n=1, Sun Room Star Left n=1, Mini Tree Star n=2, Window Star Outlines n=2). They lit up at all only because the whole-house shader bed underlays everything, which is exactly what he observed. Added `Stars` and `Window Stars` to `MUST_HAVE_GROUPS`: 0 -> 334 effects. **This is now the THIRD time (C9s/Floods, Mini Trees, Stars) that "prop X seems unused" turned out to be "prop X's group loses the weighted trim every single run." When he says a prop is unused, dump the cast first -- it is more often absent than under-treated.**

**Cone-shaped props get their own vocabulary** (`CONE_SHAPED`). "A tree is specifically round and cone shaped and there are different things that work well for it... we can apply the same concepts to the tunnel, I usually treat the tunnel similarly to a mega tree." Mega Tree and Tunnel Matrix Real now draw from spirals/pinwheel/meteors-down plus shaders, and deliberately do NOT take `shared_large_key` -- the cross-canvas cohesion rule exists so flat canvases match each other, and a cone matching a flat roofline is precisely what made the tree look like "just another matrix."

**Shader layering, first use.** When a cone prop draws a shader, a slow spirals/pinwheel is layered over it on a separate layer -- the shader keeps supplying texture, the overlay supplies the wrapping motion a cone wants. A first attempt removed shaders from the cone pool entirely, which broke the layering (nothing left to layer onto) and lost a look he'd praised; corrected to roughly half shader / half cone motion.

**Whole-house shader slowed to 22% speed.** "When shaders move quickly across the whole house it makes individual props look like they're strobing." This is a SCALE effect, not a bad speed value: the same speed that reads as gentle inside one matrix is sweeping ~50 feet of facade, so each prop sees the pattern rush past. Overridden on a copy of the registered settings so round 15's per-song parameter variation is preserved.

**Gooey's "random" intro/outro was section shredding, not shader randomness.** Measured: Gooey has 57 sections of which **47 (82%) are shorter than two bars**, concentrated exactly in the first ~60s and last ~60s -- the regions reported as random -- while its praised middle is made of proper 25-37s sections. Every section boundary re-rolls palette, shared shader, active targets and roles, so the intro was re-rolling the entire show about once per second. For contrast, The WhatsApp Shakespeare and Too Slow have ZERO sub-2-bar sections, which is why this only ever showed up on Gooey. Fixed by merging sections shorter than 2 BARS (tempo-aware, done in the generator where tempo is known rather than in the shared structure detector where it isn't): Gooey 57 -> 19 sections. **Lesson: "it looks random" can be a symptom of the orchestration UNIT being too small, not of the choices within it being bad.**

**Pre-drop blackouts no longer always total.** "Maybe it doesn't always need to go completely dark, it could just go down to outlines only." The climax drop always blacks out fully; the others alternate, and on the softened ones linear/outline props keep a dim twinkle while everything else still goes dark. Keeping every drop fully black was also making the biggest one look like all the others.

**Instrumentation headroom** -- the subtle dynamic ask: "saving some lights when some instruments drop out so we have extra lights to use when new instruments come in." Energy alone can't see this (a breakdown can stay loud while half the arrangement drops out, and RMS barely moves), but counting how many VOICES are actually playing in a section captures it directly -- free signal from the voice table built last round. ## ROUND 16 (2026-08-10): instrument VOICES -- the fix for "accurate but unreadable"

The biggest architectural change since the generator was written. Direct feedback: the rhythm was landing but not *communicating* --

> "there are lots of other flashes and patterns that I think might be going off other stems but it can just seem chaotic... I want a person watching to see some prop doing a rhythm and have a second to hear that oh it's doing that to the hi hats or to the piano chords, wow what a great detail."

**Root cause: a whole demucs stem is not an instrument.** The PULSE role was driven by `analysis.stem_analysis.stems["drums"].onset_times` -- one flat onset list conflating kick AND snare AND hats AND fills. A prop driven from that represents "the drums in general," which is not a thing anyone can lock onto. Every previous round had been improving the *timing accuracy* of that list (beat-grid filtering, real kick onsets) without touching the fact that the list itself was unreadable.

**New module `custom_tools/instrument_voices.py`:**
- **Band-splits the drums stem** into `kick` (20-150Hz), `snare` (180-450Hz), `hat` (6-11kHz), plus `bass`/`chord`/`vocal` from the other stems. Verified musically sensible on real songs: Gooey came out kick @1.5 beats IPI, snare @1.0 (a literal backbeat), hat @0.5 (eighth-note texture).
- **Snaps onsets to the sixteenth grid** rather than dropping off-grid ones (the older approach). Detector jitter of a few ms was itself part of "not quite tight."
- **Per-voice rate limits** (`MIN_GAP_BEATS`), strongest-hit-wins within each cluster so thinning keeps the accented hits a listener follows, not whichever came first. Hats capped at eighths, not sixteenths -- sixteenths at 120-130 BPM is ~8-9Hz, far past the stated 2-4Hz flash ceiling.

**`VOICE_PROP_PREFS` + `assign_voices_to_props()`**: each voice gets ONE dedicated prop for the WHOLE song. Held song-wide on purpose -- an association needs several seconds plus repeats before it lands, so re-rolling per section would guarantee it never does.

**Two measured corrections during implementation, both worth remembering:**

1. **Prominence beats member count.** A first version sorted candidates by `-t["n"]` and assigned the hi-hats to *"GE Insane Cane Half Shell Up GRP"* and the bassline to *"GE XLS Outline GRP"* -- both genuinely large multi-member `single_line` targets (OUTLINE_NAME_HINTS forces decorated-prop spoke/petal families into that category), but both are fine sub-details ON one prop. Nobody can think "ah, the hi-hats are on the half-shell-up group." Added a `prominence()` score (hero/large/must-have = 3, ordinary group = 2, submodel or `*GRP*`/outline family = 0) gating assignment at >= 2. Result: recognisable props -- Candy Canes, C9s, Floods, Orbs, Mini Trees, Megatree Star.

2. **A voice prop must be RESERVED, or the feature does nothing.** With assignment working but the prop still eligible for every other pass, direct measurement showed **only 16-37% of the effects landing on a voice prop actually coincided with its own instrument's onsets** -- the other 60-80% came from feature motion, whole-display sweeps, arpeggio chases, stab pools. A prop following its instrument a third of the time is not legible as that instrument at all. Fixed by (a) forcing `role = ROLE_PULSE` for any voice prop in PASS 1 so it never gets handed generic motion, and (b) excluding voice props from all eight generic pools (`cast_by_x/y`, kick anchors, `stab_pool`, `mel_pool`, `bass_pool`, `sway_pool`, `fill_pool`). **Re-measured: 78-89% on its own instrument.** *General lesson: a "dedicate X to Y" feature is only real if you also remove X from everything else that writes to it -- and the way to know is to measure the resulting attribution, not to assume the assignment took.*

**Drum fills (`detect_drum_fills`), designed from measurement rather than intuition.** "Drum fills are actually really great to highlight, but other random snare beats can be chaotic." Counting onsets across the whole drums stem does NOT work on modern masters -- per-bar totals were nearly flat (Gooey median 8, max 13, never reaching any sensible ratio) because a steady hi-hat pattern dominates the count. Restricting to the snare/tom band gives real separation. Validated against musical structure, not just eyeballed: on `12 to 12` it fires at 17.3s/32.9s/48.5s, spaced 15.6s apart, which at 123 BPM is **exactly 8 bars** -- it independently rediscovered the song's phrase length. It also correctly returns **zero** for Gooey (programmed bass music, genuinely constant drums) instead of inventing fills. Fills are the one place rapid activity is allowed (they bypass rate limiting) and are rendered as a hit-by-hit roll across X-ordered props, so the fill physically travels across the facade.

**The old cymbal-detail pass was deleted, not adapted** -- scattered isolated cymbal hits onto a *rotating* target, i.e. a different prop each time, on hits that weren't the focal point. That is verbatim the "random snare beats can be chaotic" problem; hats now live on one dedicated prop instead.

**Melody -> physical position** (`extract_melody_notes`): "if there are 8 orbs they can represent 8 notes." Uses pyin on the **vocals** stem specifically -- the one reliably MONOPHONIC source in a demucs split (an earlier note recorded pyin failing on polyphonic material, which is true of `other`/chords but not an isolated vocal). Notes are ranked by pitch into degrees, and degree maps to member index ordered by X, so the same note always lights the same prop and a repeated phrase draws the same shape. Cached to `melody.json` -- pyin over a full track is ~1-2 min and fully deterministic.

**Shader change rate (the Gooey intro complaint).** "The shaders keep changing and the changes are not on rhythm so it looks random." The change points were ALREADY on downbeats -- verified the bar grid is accurate (detected downbeats sit 3.96 beats apart). The problem was the RATE: a shader restarted every single bar (2.76s in Gooey) never gets far enough into its own animation to read as a developing pattern, so consecutive bars look like unrelated churn. Now held 4 bars for large canvases, 2 elsewhere. *Lesson: "not on rhythm" can mean "on the grid but too often to perceive as intentional" -- check the rate before assuming the grid is wrong.*

**Whole-house shader bed**: a shader on the real 504-member `ALL Display` group at L_BASE with Per Preview buffering, only in sections above 0.5 energy, held 8 bars per change. This also fixed a long-standing measured gap -- the sequences had been consistently *under*-lit versus the human benchmark (26-38% vs ~50%); with the bed they land at 42-53%, i.e. at the human level for the first time.

All four songs regenerated and re-rendered clean: Gooey v10 (0.4%/1.8% red, 42% lit), Too Slow v10 (0.2%/1.0%, 48%), The WhatsApp Shakespeare v5 (0.3%/6.8%, 53%), 12 to 12 v4 (0.4%/3.6%, 46%).

## ROUND 15 (2026-08-10): shaders DO track the assigned palette -- proven, not assumed -- and every other parameter never varied at all

Direct question: "we're reusing the same shaders a lot... is it time to write some of our own to have variety?" Audited the actual inventory before answering: 23 real `.fs` files exist, only 10 in use. 6 more need an input image we don't supply (the "Warp" distortion family). 2 (Circle Tunnel, Zoom Loop) were already excluded for uncontrolled colour. That left exactly 5 genuinely available and unused (Black Cherry Cosmos, Fly Through, Great Ball of Fire, Space Spinning, Target Circle) -- so the answer was "use what's already there first," not "write new ones," and Krists agreed.

**Before adding anything, settled a real open question empirically: do these shaders even respond to the assigned xLights palette?** Most of them (everything except Hex 3D Spiral) declare NO colour-type ISF input at all, which raised the possibility that palette assignment was a complete no-op for them -- a much bigger problem than "not enough variety" if true. Tested directly: found the same shader (SearchingForSomething.fs, then confirmed again with Metablob Starz.fs) rendered under two different palettes in an already-rendered `.fseq`, sampled the actual pixel output at both windows. **Confirmed: they DO track the palette correctly** -- a violet-led palette rendered as measured purple (mean RGB ~156,64,175), an orange-led one rendered as measured orange (~200,126,28). xLights applies a generic palette recolour to Shader effects independent of whether the shader declares its own colour input. This matters as a general method, not just this one answer: **when a system's behavior under an unverified assumption is genuinely unknown, render it and sample the actual pixels rather than reasoning from the ISF header alone** -- the header only says what inputs exist, not what xLights does with the ones that are absent.

**Given colour already varies correctly, the real reason multiple songs' shaders looked similar was that every OTHER parameter was frozen at the shader's ISF default, identically, on every single placement, in every song, forever.** `register_shaders()` called `shader_settings(fs, speed=400)` with zero randomization -- same rotation speed, same zoom, same intensity, every time "Wisps" (or any shader) was used anywhere. Fixed by adding a `jitter_rng` parameter to `shader_settings()` (in `isf_shader.py`): every float input not already pinned by a safety `override` gets varied around its ISF default, scaled to that input's own MIN/MAX span (so a default of exactly 0 -- common for offset/scroll-type params -- still gets real variation instead of `jitter * 0`). Params matching motion-related name hints (`rate`, `speed`, `rotat`, `zoom`, `scroll`, `cycle`, `freq`, `loop`) get a wider jitter span (45% of the input's range) than shape/density params (22%), since pace/movement was the specific thing asked for ("adding more movement... changing the parameters"). Seeded per SONG (not per placement) via `random.Random(f"{SONG_NAME}|shader-params")` -- a given shader has one consistent "personality" for the whole song (visually coherent within it) but a genuinely different one per song (verified: the same shader's settings differ meaningfully across three different songs' seeds). Safety overrides (like InnerDimensionalMatrix's pinned `flash=1.0`) are excluded from jitter by construction -- `is_override` is checked before applying jitter, so a safety pin can never be randomized back toward a risky ISF default.

**Adding the 5 new shaders immediately surfaced a NEW instance of the same red-flash pattern -- and disproved the obvious fix.** `Fly Through.fs` measured a SUSTAINED ~35-40% red for its whole active window under the `xmas` palette, shared across 7 large canvases. First hypothesis: `xmas`'s remaining red component (`#E82A2A`, colour 3) was still too saturated -- softened it to a muted `#B33A3A` berry tone. **That barely moved the number.** Re-checked and found `Vortex-V2.fs` -- one of the ORIGINAL 10 shaders, in the pool since round 11 with zero prior complaints -- doing the IDENTICAL thing under the SAME palette, completely independent of the new shader or the palette edit. Confirmed by direct measurement that softening the base colour had almost no effect on either shader's rendered redness: **these two shaders' own internal contrast/saturation math amplifies whatever colour they're fed well past the base colour's actual saturation** (`#B33A3A` has G=58, which fails even a strict "pure red" pixel test as a flat colour -- yet the rendered output still measured 35-40% red). Root cause is in the shader's own rendering, not in what colour it starts from. Excluded both `Fly Through` and `Vortex-V2` from `SHADER_PICKS` -- same low-cost, high-certainty precedent as Circle Tunnel/Zoom Loop. Confirmed clean afterward: `12 to 12` went from 7.1% mean / 49.7% peak red back to 0.3% / 4.2%. **Lesson: when a plausible fix (softening the palette) doesn't move the measured number, that itself is evidence the diagnosis was wrong -- re-measure before assuming the fix worked, and be willing to abandon a hypothesis a second data point (an established, previously-clean shader doing the same thing) already contradicts.**

**One shader kept in the pool despite elevated (but bounded) red, on purpose: `Great Ball of Fire.fs`.** Measured 15-27% red on `The WhatsApp Shakespeare` -- noticeably higher than the near-zero baseline other songs now show, but still inside the human-measured band (mean ≤11%, peak ≤27%; Blinding Lights' own peak is 27.5%). Root cause: it's a fire-simulation shader with NO ISF colour input declared at all (so it passes `has_uncontrolled_color()`, which can only check DECLARED colour inputs), but "fire" is presumably baked into its GLSL colour math regardless -- a category of risk the existing safety check structurally cannot catch, since there's no ISF signal to check against for a shader that hardcodes colour with zero exposed hook. Left it in deliberately: it stays under the established gate, and a fire shader rendering warm is arguably correct behavior, not a bug. Flagging as a known category gap in `has_uncontrolled_color()` rather than papering over it -- a future shader with this exact shape (no declared colour input, but internally colour-fixed) could still fail the gate the way Fly Through/Vortex-V2 did, and would need the same render-and-measure treatment, not just a header check, to catch.

**Regenerating the other two in-progress songs (Gooey, Too Slow) with the same code immediately caught a second instance of exactly the gap just flagged.** `Great Ball of Fire` had been kept in the pool because its elevated-but-under-gate red on `The WhatsApp Shakespeare` (15-27%) was judged as "a fire shader looking like fire, not a bug" -- explicitly flagged as unverified beyond that one song. Regenerating `Gooey` and `Too Slow` measured it at 36-40% red on BOTH, and critically, one of those was under `ice` -- a cold blue/white palette with zero red in it at all. A shader producing 40% measured red under a blue-led palette cannot be palette-driven; it confirms the shader hardcodes fire-orange/red into its own GLSL colour math with no ISF hook to override, the exact failure shape `has_uncontrolled_color()` cannot detect (it only checks DECLARED ISF colour inputs). Excluded it, regenerated all four in-progress songs, re-rendered all four: **0.1-0.4% mean red, 0.9-4.2% peak, across every song** -- the cleanest and most consistent result yet. **Lesson, worth internalizing as a standing practice: a shader (or any change) that passes on ONE song is not verified -- it is untested against every song it hasn't touched yet.** "Kept because it stayed under the gate on this one case" is a weaker claim than it sounds; regenerating the full in-progress set after any shader-pool change is the actual verification step, not a nice-to-have follow-up.

Net shader roster after this round: 8 of the original 10 (minus Vortex-V2), plus 3 of the 5 newly added (minus Fly Through, Great Ball of Fire) = 11 usable shaders, all with per-song parameter variation, all re-verified clean via render across all four songs currently in progress (Gooey v9, Too Slow v9, The WhatsApp Shakespeare v4, 12 to 12 v3).

## ROUND 14 (2026-08-10): a shared-library bug, not a generator bug -- song structure detection can silently collapse a whole song into one section

Fourth song, `12 to 12` (sombr). `run_analysis.py` reported only 6 sections, one of them a single "chorus" block spanning 0.7s to 235.9s -- essentially the entire 243-second song -- followed by five ~1.3s "transition" slivers. Generating from that would have meant one palette, one density band, one everything for the whole track: the exact "wall-to-wall sameness" failure this whole project exists to fight, just introduced one architectural layer above where it's usually caught (in `src/xlights_mcp/audio/structure.py`, shared MCP server library code used by every song, not `generate_song.py`).

**Root cause, confirmed by direct measurement:** `detect_structure()`'s novelty-boundary detector found 13 boundaries -- comfortably over the `min_sections = max(4, duration/30) = 8` threshold that gates whether the energy-based fallback kicks in -- but 11 of those 13 were crammed into the final 6 seconds of fade-out (a genuine, large spectral change, just a narrow one). The preceding 235 seconds, 97% of the song, had ZERO internal boundaries and became one section. **The bug: the fallback trigger checked boundary COUNT, never boundary DISTRIBUTION.** A detector can satisfy "found enough boundaries" while leaving almost the entire song uncovered, and nothing was checking for that.

Fixed by also computing the largest gap between consecutive boundaries (with the song's start/end as implicit bounds) and triggering the energy-based fallback -- merged in, not a full replacement, so the genuinely-detected fade-out boundaries survive -- whenever that gap exceeds `max(45s, 25% of duration)`. Confirmed: re-running structure detection standalone went from 1 section (in the affected 235s) to 15 in that same span.

**Before accepting the fallback's output, checked whether it was itself faithful rather than assuming any change from "1 section" was automatically better.** The energy-based fallback labelled nearly everything "chorus" (energy 0.78-1.00) with no verse/bridge variety at all. Rather than treat that as a second bug, measured the song's raw RMS directly in 10-second buckets: mean RMS sits in a tight 0.30-0.43 band for the entire ~230s body, only dropping at the literal fade-out. **This track genuinely has almost no loudness-based dynamic range** -- a heavily-compressed, consistently-loud modern master, not a verse/chorus dynamics song. The uniform "chorus" labelling is an honest description of the audio, not a labelling failure; whatever structural variety this song has lives in melody/harmony (chroma), not RMS, which the energy-based fallback was never built to capture. **Lesson: when a fix changes a detector's output, independently verify the NEW output is correct too, especially by going back to signal you can inspect directly (raw RMS, in this case) rather than only checking that a symptom (section count) improved.**

Generated cleanly despite the flat energy profile: 98% kick coverage (a genuinely heavy, consistent beat matching the consistent loudness), 1 real pre-drop implosion (still correctly identified as the climax), occupancy still varying 32-217 across the song (driven by escalation/rotation and the outro's real energy drop, not by section-labelled dynamics alone), 0.6% mean / 6.7% peak red. One residual, rare, and not-chased-further artifact: a single 48ms cluster of 3 arches firing together around 105.5s, on real kick onsets during what's very likely a fill/roll in the drums -- one occurrence across hundreds of cascade placements in a 98%-kick-coverage song, not a reintroduction of the systemic PULSE-role bug (those hits weren't isolated/off-beat, they were real, just densely spaced).

## ROUND 13 (2026-08-09): a "recurring" bug that was actually a second, unfixed instance -- and a real genre-classification lesson

Feedback on `The WhatsApp Shakespeare (v2)`: "too much strobing and flashing again... especially the orbs and floods... if they're not on a main beat it doesn't make sense." Also: intro too busy (wanted a real "quiet build, then the kick drops in and it jumps into gear" moment), the four-on-the-floor call was technically triggered (84% coverage) but wrong in feel ("it's not really four on the floor, it's more of an r&b beat"), the arch cascade was liked but "wasn't aligned with the kick drum," "we shouldn't always do the kick drum on the arches, we can pick other props too," and a request for more shader/layered complexity without losing the existing rhythmic feel. Chorus drops: "excellent," no notes.

The PULSE role's own comment read "short hits on drum onsets (or beats), thinned so it stays punchy" and a second comment nearby claimed onsets were "thinned to the beat grid" -- but the actual code only filtered onsets to the section's TIME WINDOW, never to the beat grid itself. On a steadier house track this went unnoticed (most drum-stem onsets land near a beat anyway); on this R&B track, raw drum-stem onsets include every ghost note and hi-hat, most NOT on a felt beat, and PULSE role fired on all of them. **Orbs and Floods fell through `wants_motion()` on a category technicality.** Both are `cat="matrix"` (Orbs n=10, Floods n=13) -- `wants_motion`'s size clause only checked `cat=="custom"`, so two decorative-but-substantial props stayed eligible for every flash-producing pass (stab_pool, and residually the newly-fixed PULSE role) purely because of which category string they happened to carry, not their actual size or standing treatment. This also happens to reinforce the STANDING preference on these two specific props from early in the project ("floods work best... big rhythmic sweeps, not constant twinkle") -- the fix wasn't in tension with prior guidance, it was restoring it.

**"It's not really four on the floor" was a real, useful genre correction -- but the underlying coverage METRIC wasn't wrong, only what the code did with a `true` answer.** This track measured 84% persistent-kick coverage, comfortably above the 80% threshold validated on a genuine house track (93%). The classification was defensible; what actually produced the "not really 4/floor, more r&b" impression was that the pulse (and separately the arch cascade) placed hits on an ASSUMED uniform beat grid regardless of what the real kick pattern did. Root fix: `detect_four_on_floor` -> `detect_kick_pattern`, now returning the REAL kick onset times (a proper `onset_detect` on the low-passed drum envelope, not just a coverage score), used as the placement source for both the persistent pulse and the arch cascade, with the generic beat grid only as a fallback for stretches with no reliable kick. A syncopated R&B pattern and a straight four-on-the-floor pattern both now render as themselves. **Lesson: "is X present" (a binary/coverage classification) and "what does X actually look like" (its real timing) are separate questions -- a detector that only answers the first one and then has a caller assume an idealized shape for the second is a bug waiting for a genre where that assumption breaks.**

**Same root cause explained "wasn't aligned with the kick drum" on the arch cascade** -- it was timed on `sec_beats` (the generic librosa beat grid), not the real kick pattern. Switched to the same `kick_times_in()` helper. Also broadened cascade eligibility from `cat=="arch"` only to `cat in ("arch","single_line") AND 3<=n<=10` per "we shouldn't always do the kick drum on the arches, we can pick other props too" -- capped at 10 members after a first attempt with no cap pulled in 20-50-member GE spoke/petal groups (via `OUTLINE_NAME_HINTS`'s `single_line` forcing) and inflated target count from 144 to 250 elements; the cascade's "one member advances per kick hit" mechanic was designed around a tight 4-arch cycle and reads as thin/scattered stretched across that many members, so those large groups keep their native (already-confirmed-good) chase/wave treatment instead. **Lesson, recurring from the round-12 tree-props decision: when broadening an eligibility rule, check the actual member-count distribution of what newly qualifies before shipping -- "same category" does not mean "same scale," and a mechanic tuned for n≈4 does not automatically generalize to n≈50.**

**Intro pacing: a real, if partial, fix for "story doesn't match a described structural moment that isn't a section boundary."** The energy-tier banding operates per SECTION; "the song starts slowly, then the kick drops and it jumps into gear" is a moment that doesn't necessarily land exactly on the audio-structure segmenter's section boundaries (this song's `intro` section measured energy=0.30, which is NOT sparse by the old <0.30 threshold, and the real kick entrance landed partway into the FOLLOWING `verse` section, not at its start). Fix: forced any section ENTIRELY before the first detected kick onset into a new, deliberately sparser band (8 active targets, below even the lowest existing tier), plus an explicit accent placed at the exact kick-entrance timestamp regardless of section boundaries. Known residual gap: a section that only PARTIALLY precedes the kick (this song's `verse`, starting 2.3s before the kick actually lands) still gets its full energy-based density for that whole section, so the "gear-shift" reads as a clear accent riding on top of already-present activity rather than a true silence-to-activity jump. Fixing this properly needs sub-section time-varying density, a bigger architectural change not attempted this round.

**Shader/complexity request, addressed as a WEIGHTING problem, not a pool problem.** SAFE_LARGE already contained more shader entries than built-ins by raw count; the actual issue was the round-11 "one shared choice per section" cohesion feature, which means whichever ONE key gets rolled for a section is what EVERY large canvas shows at once -- so even the built-ins' original ~40% share reads as a much more noticeable "plain" stretch now than when each canvas rolled independently. Added `SAFE_LARGE_WEIGHTED` (shaders double-counted) for the ongoing per-section choice, while leaving the climax drop's independent-per-target pool (plain `SAFE_LARGE`) untouched, since that one was explicitly called out as "excellent." **Lesson: a complaint about "too much of X" doesn't always mean X's relative frequency changed -- check whether a DIFFERENT recent change (here, the cohesion feature) altered how visible/salient X's existing frequency has become.**

## ROUND 11 (2026-08-09, v6 -> v7): cohesion vs. richness are different asks for different moments

Feedback after v6, opus session: shaders "add a lot" but too many matrices sat blank/flashing, the final drop was "just green, not enough movement... first drop is better," palette changes felt "disjointed," different large canvases running different shaders felt inconsistent, Tunnel Matrix Real "still flashing and strobing," Mini Tree 1/2 are "basically dark the whole time," and Candy Canes are "big enough to do movements... not just flash and color fade." Every one of these was verified by direct XML/render inspection before fixing -- several turned out to share a root cause.

**`wants_motion()`: generalized the "this target is too big/significant for a flat flash" rule.** Previously only `target.get("large")` (plus arches, separately) was excluded from flash-style passes. Candy Canes (`cat="custom"`, `n=8`, not `large`) fell through every one of those exclusions and was getting hit by FOUR independent flash-producing passes: the PULSE role, the four-on-the-floor anchor selection, `stab_pool` (synth stabs), and `detail_pool` (cymbal accents) -- all of which only checked `not t.get("large")`. Added `wants_motion(target) = large or cat=="arch" or (cat=="custom" and n>=4)` and swept it through all five call sites (PULSE-role redirect, 4-on-the-floor anchors, `stab_pool`, `detail_pool`, `display_sweep`'s `cast_by_x`/`cast_by_y`). Confirmed: Candy Canes went from 97 effects (74 of them scattered "On" flashes) to 29 (only the 6 legitimate implosion drop-hits remain as "On"). **General lesson, the same shape as the Ripple-in-two-places and TEXTURE/OUTLINE-missed-the-large-fix bugs from earlier rounds: a size/significance exclusion added to fix one reported target (large canvases) does not automatically cover every other target the same underlying problem applies to (decorative-but-substantial groups) -- and it needs to be swept through literally every pass that can place an isolated flash, not just the "main" one (PULSE role here).** `wants_motion()` now exists specifically so a future addition doesn't have to remember all five call sites individually.

**Tunnel Matrix Real "still strobing" -- read the shader's own GLSL source, not just its ISF header.** `InnerDimensionalMatrix.fs`'s `flash` input (ISF default 7.5 of range 0.5-10) had been flagged as a *possible* strobe risk back when it was first wired in, then never actually investigated because the rotation/blank-canvas bug was a bigger fire that round. Grepped the actual shader body this round: `flash` is used as `pow(sin(id_offset + flash*TIME)*0.4+0.6, flash)` -- both the FREQUENCY and the SHARPNESS of a per-tile flicker. At the default value that is a literal strobe control, not decorative texture, and it was the one shader in the whole pack with anything like it (checked all 11 others' source for `flash`/`strob`/`blink` -- zero hits). Overridden to `flash=1.0` (near the ISF minimum) via `isf_shader.py`'s new `overrides` parameter on `shader_settings()`. **General lesson: when a shader has a parameter whose ISF NAME alone sounds risky ("flash", "strobe", "blink", "rate"), read the actual `.fs` GLSL body to see how it's used before trusting the ISF default -- the JSON header only tells you the value range, not what the value does.**

**Palette cohesion vs. climax contrast are opposite asks that both needed solving, not a single compromise.** "We keep changing color palettes each section, it's a bit disjointed... limit the palette a little" -- round 9's fix (advance every section, never repeat back-to-back) was itself the overcorrection from round 8's opposite bug (one palette held 50 seconds); cycling through all 7 `PAL_CYCLE` palettes every section never let the show settle into a look. Fixed with `song_palette_cycle`: a fixed, per-song-random subset of 4 (`PALETTE_FAMILY_SIZE`), sampled once via `random.Random(f"{SONG_NAME}|palette-family")` and used for the whole song instead of the full 7. Real variety across sections, real cohesion within a song, still different per song.

Separately, "identify the big climaxes... where we can introduce more contrast and more textures" is the OPPOSITE instinct -- more variety, not less, at exactly one moment. Added `climax_sections`: the section containing the highest-`drop_energy` pre-drop implosion (or the single highest-energy section if no implosion was detected), which gets a dedicated `"climax"` palette (`#FFFFFF/#FFD700/#0047FF`, deliberately the highest-contrast combination available, never placed in `PAL_CYCLE` so it can't dilute into the ordinary rotation) instead of whatever the normal rotation would have handed it. Root cause of "just green, not impactful" at the identified drop: NOT a broken effect -- it was landing on `toxic`, a palette that (before this round) was 3 shades of the same green-yellow hue with no actual contrast colour in it at all (`#00FF6A #00FFD1 #CCFF00`) despite not being "red-led" by the round-9 standard. Fixed `toxic` itself too (swapped the third slot for genuine magenta). **General lesson: "doesn't lead with a bad colour" (round 9's fix) and "has real contrast" are different properties a palette can fail independently -- check the whole palette's hue spread, not just which colour is first.**

**Shared shaders across large canvases for cohesion, but NOT during the climax hit itself.** "We could share shaders across matrices... more cohesive overall" -- added a `shared_large_key = rng.choice(SAFE_LARGE)` computed once per section and threaded through every `feature_effect_for()` call for `large` targets that section (FEATURE, TEXTURE, OUTLINE roles), so every large canvas active in a section shows the SAME shader/pattern instead of each rolling independently. For the climax drop specifically, kept it the opposite: each large canvas draws independently from the shader-inclusive pool (`drop_hit_key(t, is_climax=True)`), because a single ~2s burst is exactly the one moment where every canvas showing something DIFFERENT reads as rich rather than disjointed -- the ~15-30s ongoing section around it is where sameness actually reads as coherent design rather than laziness.

**The cohesion feature immediately surfaced a shader-selection gap that had been invisible until now.** Once several large canvases ran the identical shader simultaneously, `Zoom Loop.fs` spiked measured red to ~39% of lit pixels for brief periodic (~1.8s) bursts during one section -- confirmed via `verify_render.py`, isolated to exactly that shader's active window. Root cause: `Zoom Loop.fs` (and `Circle Tunnel.fs`, same shape) declare a `color` TYPE ISF input (`colorInput` / `POINT_COLOR_A`+`POINT_COLOR_B`) with no `uColMode`-style palette-substitution fallback -- `isf_shader.py` correctly leaves `color` inputs unset (there's no safe generic xLights setting for them), which is fine when a shader is the only thing using its own unthemed colour at any given moment, and became visible exactly when cohesion made many canvases show it at once. Both removed from `SHADER_PICKS`; `isf_shader.py` gained `has_uncontrolled_color()` so a future shader addition is checked against this automatically rather than requiring another red-flash hunt to rediscover it. **General lesson: a change that increases how much of the display shows the SAME thing at once (cohesion, symmetry, shared choices) can take a previously-negligible per-target quirk and multiply it into a real problem -- re-verify red/contrast metrics after any change that concentrates rendering, not just after changes that add new content.**

**Mini Trees were entirely absent from the cast, not just quiet.** "I don't think we're using the mini trees at all... basically dark the whole time" -- confirmed by directly dumping `build_cast()`'s output: the `"Mini Trees"` group (Mini Tree 1 + 2) never made it into the cast pool at all (a *different* group, `"Mini Tree Star"`, did). At only 2 members it lost every weighted-sample trim against larger groups, every single run (deterministic per-song seed, so this wasn't chance -- it was a permanent, guaranteed loss). Added to `MUST_HAVE_GROUPS`. Confirmed in v7: 85-91 effects, no longer dark. **Same failure shape as the `C9s`/window-matrix-trio must-have bugs from earlier rounds -- a small group competing in the general weighted trim will lose to bigger groups with mathematical certainty if nothing exempts it, "sometimes missed" isn't the right mental model for that mechanism.**

Fixed: both roles now give `large` targets `feature_effect_for()` (the same safe animated pool FEATURE uses) instead of a static fill. **Pattern to remember: a fix applied to some roles (FEATURE, PULSE, drop-hit) does not imply the other roles (TEXTURE, OUTLINE) got it too -- when a `target.get("large")` branch exists in one place, grep for every OTHER place effects get chosen and check each one individually, the same lesson as the Ripple-in-two-code-paths bug from round 8.**

**String-sway direction, now driven by the riff's actual note rhythm.** Precise correction: "the strings riff starts on beat 2 of the 4-beat bar and should go 1-left-right-left-right-2-3-left-right-2-3-4 where numbers stay moving the same direction." Read against `detect_legato_phrase`'s existing short/long note classification, this maps directly onto a rule the sway didn't have: a **long** note (a numbered downbeat) continues the sway in whatever direction it was already travelling; a **short** note (one of a quick alternating pair) reverses direction, every time. The previous version flipped direction on every note by index parity regardless of duration -- a fixed pendulum with no relationship to which notes were actually the held beats vs. the quick ornaments, which is why it didn't read as the described rhythm even once the sway itself became visible (round 9's zone-based rebuild fixed *that* it was visible, not that its rhythm was right). Implementation: persistent `direction` variable, flips only on short notes, steps the zone index by ±1 (bounded, with an edge-bounce override since the sway obviously can't overshoot the facade). Not independently re-verifiable without watching xLights -- this is a best-faith translation of a precise but still verbally-described rhythm, flagged as such.

**Four-on-the-floor pulse and shader parameters were untouched and reconfirmed clean this round** -- v6 still measures mean 0.6%/peak 12.2% red (unchanged from v5), so none of this round's fixes reintroduced the palette bug.

## ROUND 9: read this before trusting anything above it that predates it

### We can render headlessly and measure the actual output. Do this every time.

The single biggest capability unlock of the whole project (2026-08-09). xLights ships a CLI at `/Applications/xLights.app/Contents/MacOS/xLights`:

```
-r,  --render           render sequence(s) to .fseq and exit
-hl, --headless         same, no window
-cs, --checksequence    run xLights' own sequence checker
-st, --shadertranslate  assemble every .fs in the show dir to GLSL, and on
                        macOS compile each to a Metal pipeline -- a real
                        shader validator
-s,  --show=<dir>       show directory
```

`custom_tools/fseq_reader.py` parses the resulting `.fseq` (v2, zstd-compressed, sparse channel ranges) back into real per-frame pixel values, and `custom_tools/verify_render.py` wraps render + analysis into one command:

```
python custom_tools/verify_render.py "Song (v5).xsq"
```

**Run it on every generated sequence before reporting it as done.** Until this existed, the only feedback channel was Krists opening xLights and describing what he saw, which is exactly how a self-inflicted colour bug survived four rounds of misdiagnosis (below). `zstandard` was pip-installed into the venv for this; the venv had no `pip`, so `python -m ensurepip` first.

### The "red flash render bug" never existed. It was our own palette.

Four rounds were spent hunting an xLights render/missing-asset failure on large props, progressively banning Ripple/Galaxy/Curtain/Butterfly from large canvases and rewriting buffer-style logic. **All of it was chasing a phantom.** Measuring the rendered fseq showed:

| | red as % of lit pixels |
|---|---|
| Silent Night (human) | mean 2.3%, peak 7.7% |
| Blinding Lights (human) | mean 11.2%, peak 27.5% |
| Pink Pony Club (human) | mean 0.3%, peak 5.4% |
| **generated v4** | **35-68% sustained for ~50 continuous seconds** |

The red intervals mapped *exactly* onto the sections the palette cycle had assigned `xmas` (`#FF0000` first colour) and `ember` (`#FF2A00` first) — 1:09-1:59, precisely where Krists reported "the mega tree is red around 1:13" and "the tree and garage roof are red for a while around 1:40". Most effects (On, Shockwave, single-colour chases) render predominantly the FIRST palette colour, so a palette leading with saturated red paints an entire section's large props solid red. Every drop also hardcoded `PALETTES["ember"]` across the whole cast, flashing the full display red at every drop.

Fixes: no palette leads with a pure red any more (`ember` → amber `#FF7A00`, `xmas` → green-led `#00E83C` with red demoted to colour 3), added a `candy` palette, palette now advances every section with an explicit no-repeat guard (chorus #5 and #6 had both drawn `xmas`, giving ~50s of one red scheme), and drop hits use white → gold. Result: **mean 0.6%, peak 12.2% red** — inside the human band.

**Lessons worth more than the fix:**
1. **The user's own diagnosis can be confidently wrong, and mine can inherit it.** Krists said "in my experience solid red means a missing/broken asset", my earlier notes recorded "trust his diagnostic instinct", and I then spent four rounds looking for a render fault. His *observation* (red props, at these times, on these models) was perfectly accurate and was the key evidence; his *explanation* was not. Separate the two: take reported symptoms as reliable data, treat any offered mechanism as one hypothesis, and go measure.
2. **When a bug survives more than one or two fix attempts, stop fixing and go build a way to observe it.** Three narrowing fixes that each "reduced but didn't eliminate" it should have triggered that switch much earlier.
3. **Palette colour ORDER is a first-class visual parameter**, not decoration. Colour 1 is what most effects actually show.

### The density benchmark in this file was wrong — do not chase it

`verify_sequence.py`'s "mean lit fraction, human 11-26%" counts *display elements carrying an effect*, not lit pixels; a group element expands to many models at render time, so the two diverge badly. Rendered truth: human sequences light **~50% of pixels** (Silent Night 50%, Pink Pony Club 54%), while generated v5 scoring 45% on the old metric lit only **27%** of pixels. So the generated sequences have been *sparser* than Krists's own, not denser — matching his "I don't think it's too busy or too dense, don't change the ratios." The old figure is now labelled as non-authoritative in `verify_sequence.py`. **For real density, render and measure.**

### Shader parameters are per-shader — never copy a settings block between .fs files

xLights names every shader parameter after that shader's own declared ISF input: `E_<CONTROL>_SHADERXYZZY_<inputName>`. Round 8 lifted Silent Night's working block (`uContRot`/`uColMode`/`uIntensity`/`uOffsetX/Y`/`uRotate` — which belong to `LogTransSpiralSmall.fs`/`Hex 3D Spiral.fs`) and pasted it onto `Wisps.fs` (inputs `lines`/`amp`/`glow`/...), `Metablob Starz.fs` (`radius1`/`numblobs`/...) and `InnerDimensionalMatrix.fs`. Wrong `E_` keys are silently ignored, so those shaders ran with no configuration at all. This violated a rule already written in this file ("copy verbatim — never guess key names") in a way the rule didn't anticipate: verbatim copying is only safe *for the same shader file*.

`custom_tools/isf_shader.py` now parses each `.fs`'s ISF JSON header and emits correct settings at the shader's own defaults. Type mapping (derived from real xLights strings): `float`→`E_SLIDER_...` (value × 100), `bool`→`E_CHECKBOX_...`, `long`→`E_CHOICE_...` (the option *label* string), `point2D`→two sliders `<name>X`/`<name>Y`, `color`→driven by the effect palette (no setting), `image`→**shader unusable**, we have no way to supply one. Validated by round-tripping `Hex 3D Spiral.fs`, which independently reproduces exactly the parameter set Silent Night used for that shader family.

Shaders are registered dynamically in `generate_song.py` via `register_shaders()` / `SHADER_KEYS` from `<show>/Shaders/*.fs` (the show folder has its own copy — the earlier code pointed at the **2025** show's folder). 12 curated abstract generators are used. Skip `._`-prefixed AppleDouble sidecars on this volume.

**Shaders were never the red problem** — `--shadertranslate` reported all 24 assembling and compiling to Metal, "24 ok, 0 fail". On this Mac a shader rendering wrong is a parameter/authoring issue, never a compile issue.

**Can we author new shaders?** Yes, and it's now reasonably safe: ISF is plain GLSL plus a JSON header, and `--shadertranslate` gives a real compile check on this exact machine before anything ships. The residual risk is aesthetic (does it *look* good on a roofline), not "will it break" — and `verify_render.py` can measure the colour output. Not yet attempted; the 18 usable existing shaders were the cheaper win first.

### A gesture at house scale must travel across props, not repeat within them

The chorus string sway was placing an identical local `Wave` on all ~25 pool members simultaneously, every note. Krists didn't see a sway at all — 25 props each doing their own little left-right wave reads as undifferentiated shimmer. A sway is a movement of the **whole facade**: the lit mass itself has to move bodily left then right. Rebuilt as an X-sorted zone pendulum (pool split into 3-5 zones by real position, one zone lit per note, zone index walking back and forth, long notes also lighting the next zone in the direction of travel so the gesture "leans"). Same principle as the arch cascade. **General rule: for any gesture meant to read across the display — sway, sweep, chase — express it as a travelling position across props; a per-prop effect applied to many props at once never reads as one movement.**

### Genre-level structure deserves its own persistent layer

Everything in the generator was event-driven (a riser here, a stab there), so the constant underlying pulse of a dance track was never modelled by anything — hence "it's a dance song that's 4 on the floor, I don't feel the 4 on the floor in the lights." Added `detect_four_on_floor()`: isolates sub-150Hz energy from the drums stem, samples it around each beat, and scores *of the beats that carry any low end at all*, what fraction carry a strong one. Ignoring silent beats is essential — a first version measured across all beats and scored this house track at 23% because ~36% of its beats are in drum-free intros/breakdowns. Measured: **house track 0.93, Gooey (syncopated bass music) 0.58**, so a 0.80 threshold separates them with margin. When true, a persistent quarter-note pulse runs on ~8 anchor props spread evenly across the facade through every section above 0.45 energy (downbeats hit all anchors, other beats alternate halves so the pulse has internal left/right movement). Rate-safe by construction: a quarter note is BPM/60 Hz (2.2Hz here), and the pass is skipped above 4Hz. **Pattern to reuse: when a whole song is built on a repeating structural feature, detect that feature and give it its own always-on layer — don't expect event detectors to imply it.**

### Tunnel: corrected misunderstanding

`Tunnel Matrix Real` **is** the real, wired walk-through tunnel and must be sequenced as a centrepiece — it's now in `HERO_MODELS` and `LARGE_CANVAS` (65 effects in v5, was 0 in v4). The ~40-80 `Tunnel Arches N` models are the *disabled, unwired* arch-build preview: Krists modelled the tunnel as one matrix because wiring individual arches was impractical, and keeps the arch version only to see how an arch build would look. Round 8 had these exactly backwards.

## Round 8 fixes (2026-08-09, `Too Slow (All Night)` v3 -> v4)

**Shader pack found and wired in.** A real, working ISF-format GLSL shader pack (21 `.fs` files, proper headers/credited authors, not authored by me) was found on disk at `/Volumes/XLIGHTS/XLights 2025/Shaders/` — resolves the "shaders deferred, no verified local example" blocker noted in the section above. Added `shader_wisps`/`shader_blob`/`shader_matrix` effect entries pointing at real files (`Wisps.fs`, `Metablob Starz.fs`, `InnerDimensionalMatrix.fs`) using the same validated `E_SLIDER_SHADERXYZZY_*`/`E_CHOICE_SHADERXYZZY_uColMode`/`E_TEXTCTRL_Shader_*` settings structure measured from Silent Night, and added them to the large-canvas `SAFE_LARGE` pool and the `matrix`-category escalation pool. Confirmed in v4's actual XML: 251 `Shader` effect placements spread across Mega Tree (26), Garage Roof (9), Sun Room Window Matrix (indirectly via matrix pool), Curtain Front/Garage/South, Floods, Orbs, and the front window matrix trio.

**The implosion collapse phase was a second, separate Ripple leak — round 6/7's fix only covered half of it.** Round 6/7 fixed `feature_effect_for()`'s large-canvas pool to stop offering Ripple/Galaxy/Curtain/Butterfly, and separately gave Mega Tree (tree-shaped) `shockwave` instead of `ripple_implode` during the pre-drop-implosion collapse phase. But that tree-only carve-out (`collapse_key = "shockwave" if (t.get("large") and t["cat"] == "tree") else "ripple_implode"`) left every OTHER large canvas — Garage Roof, Sun Room Window Matrix — still hardcoded to `ripple_implode` on every single pre-drop implosion, completely bypassing the narrowed feature pool. **Arches sometimes strobed despite the dedicated cascade — root cause was a role-assignment gap, not the cascade itself.** The per-beat cross-model cascade (X-sorted, one member per beat, alternating direction) only actually runs when the "Arches" cast entry gets `ROLE_FEATURE` for a given section. When it got `ROLE_PULSE` instead (a real, non-synthetic group), the generic PULSE broadcast mechanism fired all 4 arches simultaneously via `add_target()` — a flash, exactly what was reported ("they all sometimes strobe or flash briefly"), and it completely bypassed the cascade despite the cascade being confirmed-good elsewhere. Fixed by extending the existing `large`-canvas PULSE exclusion (`if role == ROLE_PULSE and target.get("large")`) to also cover `target["cat"] == "arch"`. Confirmed in v4: zero instances of 3+ arches firing within 100ms of each other across the whole song (was previously unbounded).

**Arch cascade "not rhythmic" despite being beat-timed — attack shape, not timing, was the problem.** The cascade already lands exactly on the beat grid, but used `on_swell` (150ms fade-in) rather than a sharp hit. Hypothesis: a slow fade-in undercuts the perceptual sense of landing exactly on a beat even when the timestamp is correct — the ear/eye needs a sharp attack to read as "hitting" the beat, not just occurring near it. Changed to `on_hit` (~20ms fade-in). Confirmed in v4: 27 of 35 individual-arch cascade effects use the sharp 0.02s fade-in (the remaining 8 are unrelated FEATURE-role effects assigned to the group entry in other sections, not the cascade itself). Not independently verifiable as "more rhythmic-feeling" without watching xLights render — flagged as the most plausible mechanism, not a certainty.

**Submodel auto-discovery for hero props** (`select_submodels()` + a new `build_cast()` step 2a). **Tunnel/Tunnel Matrix Real deliberately deprioritized.** Direct correction: "don't confuse [the 4 garage roof arches] with the arch tunnel that's disabled because it's just for testing rendering" — the 80-arch tunnel walkthrough structure and `Tunnel Matrix Real` are not part of the actual show, so effort spent giving them guaranteed hero/must-have treatment (as in earlier rounds) is wasted.
