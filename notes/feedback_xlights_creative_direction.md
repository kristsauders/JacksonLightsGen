**Wants musical detail, not just beat-sync.** Explicitly said the MCP server's own "auto" mode (beat-driven, generic) wasn't good enough. What he asked for by name, unprompted, as things to actively look for in every song:
- Repeated/arpeggiated melodies (e.g. C-E-G-A-C-E-G-A) lit as a chase across a sequence of props (arches, orbs, etc.) following the note pattern
- Rising sound effects swept upward; broadband/filter-sweep effects swept left-to-right across the whole display
- C9s, vertical strips, and window outlines specifically called out as looking great with back-and-forth sway or up-down bounce synced to rhythm
- Musical cutouts right before a drop/section change get a meteor-down or reverse-explosion/implosion-to-black, not a plain dim or cut
- Small per-detail accents (e.g. a cymbal pattern showing on some prop) — "little details" matter to him, not just the big moments

**Why:** he wants "drama that's really tied to the music and melody and feel" — the bar is a sequence that reads as *listened to*, not templated. Apply this to every future song, not just Gooey: treat this list as a checklist of opportunities to actively hunt for before generating, not passive fallback behavior.

**How to apply:** when starting a new song, explicitly run the melody/riser/implosion/accent detection pass (see [[xlights_sequencing_technique_learnings]]) before writing any effects — don't default to the ambient-bed-only auto mode and call it done.

**Round 2 feedback (2026-08-09, after the rhythm-fix pass), more specific creative direction learned from watching v4:**
- **C9s working well** — the beat-synced back-and-forth bounce reads as clearly on-beat. Confirmed-good pattern, keep it.
- **Floods are an accent prop, not an ambient one.** He wants floods used for "big rhythmic left/right/left or top/bottom/top kinds of things" — occasional, punchy, cross-model sweeps — not constant twinkle/sparkle. Explicitly: "floods work best when the other models near that flood are off" — i.e. when a flood accent fires, physically nearby ambient models should dim/go dark so the flood sweep reads with contrast against a darker backdrop. This is a staging/choreography note, not just an effect-choice note.
- **Orbs/floods sparkle-strobe complaint**: whatever bed effect keeps them lit is "good for little moments here and there but too much all the time" — decorative small matrix/orb props should NOT get a constantly-running twinkle/strobe bed; save that texture for occasional accent moments.
- **Large matrices deserve different treatment than small ones.** He explicitly called out that the Sun Room Window Matrix and the Garage Roof matrix are "each really large and can do more complex things" — bucketing them into the same generic "matrix" pool as small orbs/curtains wastes their capability. Split large-canvas matrices out for richer effect treatment (proper shapes, more elaborate motion) rather than one-size-fits-all.
- **Garage curtain rendering as separate individual curtains instead of one combined matrix** — he suspects the generator is targeting the wrong model(s) (the 5 individual `Curtain Garage-N` panels) instead of a model meant to represent the whole garage curtain area as one canvas. Always double check for a combined/whole-area model before defaulting to a family of sub-panels.
- **Model render/list order matters and he's deliberate about it**: "the most specific models get layered on top... the lowest layers in the list... are rendered last" — he manages overlapping/nested models via list order in the xLights layout, and expects generated sequences to respect that same physical-overlap reality (some models literally share physical pixels with others).
- **Arches wanted more side-to-side cross-model sweep** (leaping-arches style, e.g. across Arch 1-4 on the garage) as a regular recurring behavior, not just a rare signature moment — noted this "often works well."
- **Overall density concern**: suspects the sequence is "just too dense" and that this itself may be part of why it doesn't feel rhythmic — fewer, more purposeful/legible movements over constant simultaneous activity everywhere.
- **Wants ongoing comparison against his existing finished sequences for technique inspiration** — treat this as a standing move, not a one-time diagnostic: when unsure how to treat a prop type well, check how an existing good sequence handled a similar prop before inventing an approach.

**Round 6 feedback (2026-08-09, on `Too Slow (All Night)` v6 -> v7), general/reusable direction on cohesion vs. contrast:**
- **Cohesion and contrast are both wanted, at different scopes.** "We keep changing color palettes each section, it's a bit disjointed... limit the palette a little" (ordinary sections want a settled, consistent look) alongside "identify the big climaxes... where we can introduce more contrast and more textures" (the one big moment wants MORE variety, not less). Don't resolve these as one compromise setting — they're genuinely different asks for different moments in the same song. Same principle applies to "we could share shaders across matrices... more cohesive overall" (ordinary sections: same shader across large canvases) vs. wanting the actual drop to feel like the peak (climax: different pattern per canvas reads as richer for a brief burst).
- **"The biggest drop should be the climax visually too. I feel like the first drop is better than the last one."** He's tracking relative impact across multiple similar moments in one song, not just whether any given moment individually looks fine — worth explicitly identifying which moment in a song IS the biggest one and making sure the treatment scales with that, not treating every implosion/drop identically.
- **Points at specific still-broken instances but frames them as reusable patterns**: "the tunnel is big and can't flash and strobe" and "candy canes... big enough to do movements" were both individual observations that generalize into "the same care applied to Mega Tree/Garage Roof needs to apply to every visually-significant prop, not just the ones already fixed." Explicitly said this round's feedback "should apply to other songs when we start more" and "we need to add more cohesion" as a standing, not one-off, quality bar.
- **Distinguishes "not working" from "not enough of it"**: mini trees being "basically dark the whole time" (existence bug) is a different complaint from candy canes "only flash and color fade" (existence fine, treatment wrong) — worth checking which one an under-used-prop complaint actually is before fixing it, since the fixes are different (get it into the cast at all, vs. change what it's allowed to do once active).
- **Asks direct capability/difficulty questions mid-feedback** ("is it hard to write more shaders?") the same way he did in round 5 — still just scoping, not requesting the work; a plain feasibility answer is what's wanted, not follow-through.

**Round 7 feedback (2026-08-09, on `The WhatsApp Shakespeare` v1 -> v2), R&B/genre-specific and reinforcing standing rules:**
- **The "no isolated flash without a rhythmic reason" rule is not a one-time fix, it's a standing constraint he'll re-flag every time it resurfaces in a new code path**: "there's too much strobing and flashing again... if they're not on a main beat then it doesn't make sense to have that flashing." Treat any report of scattered brief flashes as "go find which pass isn't beat-filtered," not as a fresh problem needing a fresh mechanism.
- **Names specific props precisely** ("especially the orbs and floods") -- always worth checking why THOSE specific props specifically, since it's usually a category/classification gap (this round: they're `matrix` cat, not `custom`, so a `custom`-only rule silently excluded them) rather than a random subset.
- **Distinguishes structural song moments from section labels**: "the song starts slowly and then when the kick drum starts that's a great moment where the song suddenly really jumps into gear" -- describing a moment tied to an audible event (the kick's entrance), not to whatever the generator's own section-boundary detector calls "intro." Worth checking whether a described moment aligns with a detected section boundary or needs its own independent detection.
- **Corrects genre/technical classifications precisely and explains the felt difference**: "it's not really four on the floor, it's more of an r&b beat." He'll accept that a detector's coverage number was defensible while still being right that the OUTPUT didn't match the genre -- the useful move is asking what a "coverage is high" verdict was then USED to do (here: assume a mechanical uniform grid) rather than second-guessing the number itself.
- **Explicitly asks for variety/non-exclusivity, not replacement**: "we shouldn't always do the kick drum on the arches, we can pick other props too" -- he liked what arches were doing, just wanted it not to be arches' exclusive job. When he says "don't always X," the fix is usually broadening eligibility/rotation, not removing X's current behavior.
- **Praises specifically, which narrows what NOT to touch**: "the chorus drops are excellent though" -- called out unprompted, mid-feedback, about a mechanism (implosion/climax handling) that had just been reworked the round before. Confirms that round's fix landed; leave that code path alone unless a new complaint touches it directly.
- **Wants MORE of something (shaders) while explicitly protecting what's already working** ("just don't lose the rhythmic movement that's there") -- when asked to increase one quality, check whether the natural implementation (e.g. swap built-ins out for shaders) would cost a DIFFERENT quality he already values, and prefer reweighting/blending over replacement.

**Round 8 feedback (2026-08-10) — the clearest statement yet of what "good" means to him:**
- **The goal is LEGIBILITY, not just accuracy.** The defining quote, worth re-reading before any rhythm work: *"I want a person watching to see some prop doing a rhythm and have a second to hear that oh it's doing that to the hi hats or to the piano chords, wow what a great detail, you know?"* Timing being correct is necessary but not sufficient — if a viewer can't attribute a prop's movement to a specific instrument, the detail is wasted. He'll describe this as "chaotic" even when every individual hit is on a real onset.
- **Explicitly accepts less rhythm in exchange for clearer rhythm**: "I don't want to lose all the rhythm but need to tighten it up so it's clear what instrument a given prop is representing. If it keeps changing too rapidly it seems random." When density and clarity conflict, clarity wins.
- **Distinguishes musically-meaningful density from noise**: drum fills are "really great to highlight" while "other random snare beats can be chaotic if they're not the song's focal point." Rapid isn't inherently bad — unmotivated rapid is. Worth asking, for any burst of activity, whether it corresponds to a moment the music itself marks.
- **Thinks in terms of prop affordances**: "if there are 8 orbs they can represent 8 notes." He sees multi-member props as having natural representational capacity, not just as things to light up. Look for that mapping when a prop has structure.
- **"Not on rhythm" may mean "too often," not "off the grid"** — his Gooey shader complaint turned out to be about change RATE, not timing accuracy (the changes were already on downbeats). Verify which before fixing.
- **Open-ended and generous with scope**: "We can always bring in more tools to do this if you find something online and we can write shaders for these things too, be creative." He'd rather see an ambitious attempt than a conservative one, and has repeatedly approved new dependencies/tools when the reason was explained.

**Round 9 feedback (2026-08-10) — refinements once the voice system landed:**
- **"Movement can also be rhythmic."** He does not equate rhythm with flashing. A prop hitting the beat by MOVING (a tree pulsing up/down, a line chasing along its length) is richer than the same prop flashing, and he wants both in the vocabulary. When a rhythm feels thin, consider whether it should be motion rather than adding more hits.
- **Unison across many props is a distinct tool from one-prop-one-instrument** — "having many of the verticals or horizontals or c9s or window outlines doing the SAME rhythmic movement can be super effective." Don't let a per-prop attribution rule crowd out ensemble gestures; they serve different purposes (attribution vs scale).
- **Prop shape should drive effect choice** — "a tree is specifically round and cone shaped and there are different things that work well for it." He treats the walk-through tunnel like the mega tree. Generic large-canvas treatment on a cone reads as lazy to him.
- **Scale changes what a speed value means**: a shader speed that's pleasant on one matrix "makes individual props look like they're strobing" when spread across the whole house. Re-tune, don't reuse, when the canvas size changes dramatically.
- **Wants graduated dynamics, not just binary drop/no-drop** — "it doesn't always need to go completely dark, it could just go down to outlines only," plus the subtler idea of "saving some lights when some instruments drop out so we have extra lights to use when new instruments come in." He thinks about lit percentage as a budget to spend for contrast.
- **Recurring pattern worth internalising**: when he says a prop "isn't being used much," check whether it's in the cast at all before assuming it needs better treatment. Three separate times now (C9s/Floods, Mini Trees, Stars) the prop's group was losing the cast trim on every single run and was simply absent.

**Round 10 feedback (2026-08-10) — prop physicality and dynamics:**
- **He distinguishes props by how light actually leaves them.** Orbs sparkling together = great; the identical treatment on floods = strobing, "because they light up the whole house." A wash light aimed at everything cannot take the same rhythm as a cluster of small point sources. When a treatment works on one prop and fails on another, ask what the prop physically does to the room, not just its size or category.
- **"Multiple spots or dots or lines can all move side to side instead of a single one going all the way across."** He reasons about apparent speed, not just effect speed — on a long run, splitting the motion into several dots keeps the tempo while calming the visual. Useful general trick for any long linear prop.
- **Wants per-prop animation on collections of separate objects** (stars especially). A sweep across scattered objects reads as sequential flashing to him, not as movement.
- **Notices axis, not just presence, of movement**: "I notice it's always side to side, we should do up and down too." Vertical props should move vertically.
- **Corrected a measurement I proposed, precisely**: voice count is insufficient because "often the voices are all there but more mellow for a second and then the bass comes in really hard." He is thinking in terms of intensity/dynamics, not instrumentation alone — worth reaching for loudness/spectral measures when he talks about energy.
- **Thinks about fills as structural signposts**: a fill precedes a new section, so dipping before it makes the section start land. He connects musical function to visual pacing naturally; take those cues literally.

**Round 19 feedback (2026-08-11) — prop-appropriateness, and the question of automation vs curation:**
- **He asked directly whether I am automating or actually making choices**: *"Are you automating the full sequence creation, or are you using all these tools you've built to build an understanding of the structure and candidates etc and then you make choices so the whole song feels cohesive? I want to make sure you're weighing in rather than purely automating it."* The honest answer was: fully automated, rules only, nothing per-song. **Answer this plainly if it comes up again — he is asking about the process, not fishing for reassurance.** The proposed next step (not yet done, he hasn't responded): write a per-song section-by-section PLAN in text, get his reaction before rendering, then have the generator execute it. That is the natural ceiling of the rules-only approach.
- **A 2D canvas should never be flat-pulsed.** "I don't like pulsing the whole mega tree though, just like pulsing matrices doesn't make sense, nor the tunnel matrix." Matrices/cones are drawing surfaces — interrupting a developing pattern several times a bar wastes them. Their OUTLINES may flash. Note this had to be fixed in TWO places: the beat-anchor pool AND `VOICE_PROP_PREFS` (the kick voice was independently giving Mega Tree 167 flashes/song). **When a "don't do X to prop type Y" note lands, grep for every pass that can pick a prop, not just the one that prompted the complaint.**
- **FLOODS, precise spec** (he has now given flood direction three separate rounds, this is the most concrete): floods are stage lighting, not an instrument. Two uses only — (a) CONTRAST: "when lights are off in a section of the house then those floods could be on"; (b) ALTERNATION: "lowers on beats 1 and 3 and uppers on beats 2 and 4," or left/right. "Floods should be used more sparingly." He offered to create xLights groups; not needed — upper/lower and left/right split cleanly from the layout's WorldPos (upper y~433-456, lower y~53-81).
- **One chase, followable, on the beat grid.** "The motion in the verticals and horizontals is too messy, there are always multiple chases and they seem to change direction at odd times with overlapping chases. I think having a single chase should work best, it can also often follow the main beat and you can follow the chase clearly." Also: C9s "sometimes moving too fast... we just don't want them moving too fast because then they're hard to follow," and direction changes belong on half bars. **Apparent followability is his real metric for motion** — same instinct as the round-10 "multiple dots instead of one" note.
- **Alternation over exclusivity, again.** "I also didn't see the window outlines or verticals doing any pulsing to the beat... We can alternate, sometimes flash them, sometimes do motion." This is the third time (see round 7's "we shouldn't always do the kick drum on the arches") that the right fix was broadening eligibility rather than swapping behaviour. **When he says a prop only ever does X, he wants X plus something else, not X replaced.**
- **Repetition of a single gesture is a defect even when the gesture is good.** "We seem to be overusing the jagged wave effect thing that moves left and right across the matrices, it's too repetitive." Root cause was a single hardcoded effect key for the chord voice, used on the same prop all song, in every song. **Any fixed `key` in a preference table is a future repetition complaint.**
- **Explicit process instruction**: don't re-render the whole show every round — "re-render like 3 songs so I can check how they're looking. We'll re-render all when we want to later." Rendering 11 songs takes long enough to matter now. Use `VER=99` (or similar) with `batch_build.sh` for throwaway review builds so his real version history stays clean.

**Process preferences:**
- Fine bringing in any external tool/dependency (websearch for technique research, installing Rust for a better demucs build) to get a better result — approved without hesitation when I explained *why* it mattered (unlocked 6-stem separation). Prefer proposing the real fix over a workaround, and explain the tradeoff briefly; he's technical enough to say yes fast.
- Iterative workflow: I generate a draft, he opens it in xLights himself to review (I have no way to preview a light show), then gives specific feedback ("this timing is off," "use a different prop for that chase") for me to adjust directly rather than regenerating from scratch.
- Wants this to scale to building a whole new show across many songs this session and future ones — treat infrastructure/tooling decisions (where scripts and generated data live, environment fixes) as investments that need to survive across sessions, not one-off scratch work.
